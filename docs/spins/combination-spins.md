# Combination Spins

A combination spin is a sequence of **two or more spins linked together by changing feet or position without stopping rotation**. Changing from one foot to the other — camel into back sit, say — is a legal combination; so is remaining on the same foot, as in a camel into a forward sit. Advanced combinations link three or more spins together to create interesting, innovative spins.

There is no feet diagram on this page, deliberately: a combination's print is the point. Done well, **the print does not move** — it is traced over and over on the same spot (see [How the change works](#how-the-change-works)).

## Common combinations

The book's list of combinations skaters commonly perform:

- Camel / back camel
- Sit / back sit, or change sit
- Camel / sit
- Camel / layback
- Camel / layback / back sit
- Flying camel / back sit — *when completed as a continuous action in the air, this combination becomes a [death drop](./flying-spins.md#death-drop)*

## How the change works

- **Open out, then pull in again.** Opening the arms and free leg between spins is imperative to continue the rotation. Each time the foot or position changes, open out — and then pull in again to keep spinning.
- **The centring does not change.** Continuity is achieved while stepping from a front to a back spin: open the arms and the free foot, place the free foot **pigeon-toed (toe to toe) in front of the skating foot**, and let the skating foot push away from the spin so the free foot is placed **on the same spot the skating foot formerly occupied**.
- Balance, control, and proper placement over the blade are necessary to achieve strong combination spins.

## Quality — what makes a good combination

- All spins in the combination **equal** in the strength of the spin and the **number of rotations** held.
- Each spin **centred, controlled, and fast** — held to the same standard as the solo spins.
- **Creativity is strongly encouraged and rewarded by the judges.**

## Where combinations go wrong

Problems occur when:

- the first spin is poorly executed,
- speed is insufficient, or
- the timing between the first and the second spin is poor.

> **Teaching tip (book):** as with combination jumps, make sure the skater can execute **each** spin of the combination with sureness and consistency. When trouble occurs in the combination, go back to the solo spins to make sure each one is solid — then check position and strength in the transition between the spins.

## Master the Move — Lucinda Ruh's dazzling spins

*The book illustrates this section with a photo of Ruh's Biellmann spin — flexibility, positioning, and centering in one frame — but the photo is covered by a black block in this copy of the book scan, so it can't be shown here.*

Switzerland's **Lucinda Ruh** is perhaps one of the best spinners in the world: she has not only mastered the technique of spinning, she has invented new and interesting positions, and her flexibility, intricacy, and speed are dazzling — earning perfect scores at the World Professional Championships. If you've seen Ruh skate, you've noticed her spins **always remain in one spot**, and the unique arm and leg positions that make spins like the layback and Biellmann even more interesting.

To reach that level, a skater needs technical precision:

- Spins should be **fast, controlled, and centred**
- The hips and shoulders should be **square**
- The skating foot should make **small, even circles**
- The body should be **balanced over the blade** to maintain momentum

Once the fundamental technical elements are perfected, put your creative mind to work and try out different body positions — improving your flexibility widens your creative options *and* improves your technique.

## Chapter summary

All skills look easy when they're done well. Spins are often underrated when compared to jumps, but without the balance spins provide in a program, a skater would be boring to watch and the program incomplete. In recent years spins have begun to receive welcome attention, and skaters are now being rewarded for the difficulty and ingenuity of their spins.
