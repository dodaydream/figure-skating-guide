# Layback Spins

The layback — an upright spin with the body **arched straight back from the waist** — is, in the book's words, one of the most beautiful of all spins. This page pairs it with its athletic variation, the **Biellmann**: a cross between the layback and the catch foot camel, pulling the free blade up overhead into a near split. Both are flexibility spins — the book's practice notes (backbends, barrier work) are part of the technique, not an extra. Master the [Spinning Fundamentals](./spins.md) first.

Two spins:

- [Layback Spin](#layback-spin) — the classic arch, free leg in attitude behind
- [Biellmann Spin](#biellmann-spin) — the free blade pulled up above the head

> **Reading the feet diagrams:** three keyframes — **1 · Entry, 2 · The position, 3 · Exit** — looking down at your own feet. In the layback stage the dashed free boot sits **directly behind** the spinning boot; in the Biellmann it is pulled up behind the head, which from above tucks it in close behind the spinning boot. The red dot marks the edge doing the work; pale blue is the print the blade leaves on the ice.

## Layback Spin

_The standard layback is executed on one foot while the body bends straight back from the waist and the arms are placed overhead in a graceful position. Side laybacks are an easier alternative and are frequently done._

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | The arch builds **from the hips upward, one vertebra at a time**: the hips first move slightly forward of the skating foot, then the bend rolls up through the waist and spine until the neck and head incline back too. As the body leans back, the **skating hips move forward as a counterbalance** to the arch. Arm positions are optional — both arms overhead, one up and one down, or both crossed in front of the chest — and creative variations are encouraged and rewarded by judges. |
| **Feet** | From the upright spin position, the upper body turns away from the right (free) leg until it sits behind the left (skating) leg in an open, turned-out position — like a ballet **attitude**, but the thigh is not placed as high. The ideal placement: the free foot **directly behind the skating foot**, with the free knee at the same height as the free foot, or slightly higher. |
| **Blade** | The standard upright-spin edges — the setup is the forward scratch spin's: step in on a left forward outside edge, hook, and spin balanced forward. |
| **Preparation** | Exactly the same setup as for a **forward scratch spin**, except that the free foot begins in front and is then placed behind the skater as the layback position forms. |
| **Exit** | Reverse the entry, slowly: starting with the **head and neck**, lift one vertebra at a time, top to bottom, back to fully upright. The right (free) leg comes around to the front, and the spin finishes in a **forward scratch spin** — the same as for all other spins. |

**Tips**

- Practice **backbends on the floor** to increase flexibility, and hold the rink barrier to feel the layback position before spinning it.
- The book notes ladies perform the layback more frequently than men, although a few men have perfected lovely laybacks.

## Biellmann Spin

_Named after its creator, Swiss champion **Denise Biellmann**. An athletic variation and cross between the layback spin and the catch foot camel, requiring great flexibility: reach back for the blade of the free foot and pull the free leg overhead in a nearly split position, while remaining upright over the skating foot._

_No dedicated Biellmann technique tutorial exists on the preferred channels (their Biellmann videos cover stretching only), so this card follows the disclosed-line practice used elsewhere in the guide._

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | Balanced **forward on the blade**, much the same as for a layback spin. At full stretch, both elbows extend and the **head lies back** to achieve maximum stretch. |
| **Feet** | From the forward open spin position, the knee of the right (free) foot bends behind; the free foot is pulled up **behind and then above the head**. |
| **Blade** | Forward scratch edges — the setup is exactly the same as for a forward scratch spin: from back crossovers, hold the last right back inside edge with the left (free) leg extended behind in line, then step into the circle on a strongly curving **left forward outside edge**, knee well bent, shoulders level, weight over the skating hip. |
| **The catch** | Reach the **right hand back for the blade of the free foot, palm facing upward**, and pull the foot up behind the head; then the **other hand grabs the blade as well** — both hands on the blade — and keep lifting until the foot is above the head. *(Variation: move the free leg to the front or side of the body instead of pulling it up from behind.)* |
| **Exit** | Release the blade and return to an upright position; the spin pulls in and finishes **exactly as a forward scratch spin**. |

**Tips**

- **Flexibility is key:** have a solid layback first, and master the layback exercises, before attempting the Biellmann.
- Practice off the ice before trying it on the ice, and make sure your body is **fully warmed up**; once the position is safe, the spin itself doubles as a stretch.
