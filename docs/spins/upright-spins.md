# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

Upright spins are performed with the body upright over the skating foot — the basic spins on which all the others are formed. From Chapter 7 of *The Complete Book of Figure Skating*. The shared fundamentals — entry, centring, arm positions, speed, exit — live on the [Spins](./spins.md) page and apply to every spin here.

::: tip Go back to the uprights
When problems occur in more advanced spins, go back to the basic uprights and correct the errors there — they are usually found at this level. Nothing can replace a solid foundation.
:::

::: info Forward spin or back spin?
"Forward" is a misnomer: a **forward spin** actually spins on a **back inside edge**, on the **left foot** (counter-clockwise). A **back spin** spins on the **back outside edge** of the **opposite, right foot**. All descriptions here are counter-clockwise; reverse them if you rotate the other way.
:::

## Which spin when?

- [The first spin anyone learns → Two-Footed Spin](#two-footed-spin)
- [Your first one-foot spin → One-Footed Spin](#one-footed-spin)
- [One foot, free leg held wide → Forward Open Spin](#forward-open-spin)
- [The same open position, on the back outside edge → Back Open Spin](#back-open-spin)
- [Pull in tight and finish fast → Forward Scratch Spin](#forward-scratch-spin)
- [The fastest basic spin — and the jump air position → Back Scratch Spin](#back-scratch-spin)
- [Two feet, crossed → Cross Foot Spin](#cross-foot-spin)

How to read the diagrams: each spin is shown in three keyframes — **1 · Entry, 2 · The spin, 3 · Exit** — looking down at your own feet. The entry print is a curve whose diameter diminishes as it spirals into the centre; the spin stage sits centred over the tight print at the middle; the exit glides out on a back outside edge. Red dot = the edge doing the work; pale blue = the print left on the ice; a dashed boot is the free foot, lifted off the ice. Two exceptions: the **two-footed spin** starts in place, so its stages are Start / Pull in / The spin, and in the **cross foot spin** both boots are on the ice in the spin stage — that is the spin.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Start with knees bent and both arms over to the **right** side; swing the arms and shoulders across to the **left** as the knees straighten — the body follows the arms until hips and shoulders are **square**. Once balanced, pull the hands together in front of the body, then draw the arms in, **elbows up**, body square. |
| **Feet** | Two feet, about **shoulder-width apart**. For a beginner, toes slightly **turned in**, forming a triangular platform to spin on. |
| **Blade** | Starts on the **flat** of the blades. As you advance, feel the weight split: left foot toward the **ball** (left back inside edge), right foot toward the **heel** (right forward inside edge). |

#### Tips

- This is where the pull-in is learned: hands meet first, *then* the arms draw in.
- Keep the elbows up as you pull in, or the spin wobbles off its centre.

::: info The book's feet for this one
The book describes this introductory spin on the **right** foot — step in on the right forward outside edge, hook onto the right back inside, exit onto the left back outside — unlike the left-foot forward spins below. The description is followed as printed.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Shoulders level, body weight aligned over the skating hip. Arms out to the side during the spin; as the free foot draws in, the arms draw in too, increasing momentum. |
| **Feet** | Begin on the left back inside edge, right leg extended behind in line with the skating foot; step into the circle on a strongly curving **right forward outside edge**, knee well bent. The left (free) leg swings from back to front, low at first, rising to a stretched position in front at about skating-hip height — then it is brought straight in to **rest beside the skating knee**. |
| **Blade** | The three turn on the skating foot hooks the spin — balance forward on the blade, catching the first tooth of the toe pick on a **back inside edge** — as the skating knee straightens. |
| **Exit** | The free foot slides slowly down the skating leg until its toe rests beside the skating boot; then it is placed on the ice and pushes off onto a **left back outside edge**. Hold the finish. |

#### Tips

- Learn the balance from a two-footed spin: spin slowly on two feet, lift one foot, bend the free knee, and place the free foot beside the skating knee.
- Don't try it at full speed — a slow spin you can balance teaches more than a fast one you fall out of.

## Forward Open Spin {#forward-open-spin} <Badge type="warning" text="Beginner–Intermediate" />

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Shoulders level, weight over the skating hip. The arms remain **out to the side** in an open position for the whole spin — nothing pulls in. |
| **Feet** | From back crossovers: hold the last right back inside edge, left (free) leg extended behind in line with the skating foot; step into the circle on a strongly curving **left forward outside edge**, knee well bent. | **Blade** | The three hooks the spin on a **back inside edge**, balance forward, catching the first tooth of the toe pick, as the skating knee straightens. |
| **Exit** | The free leg lowers to the toe of the spinning foot, steps down, and pushes off onto a **right back outside edge**. Hold the finish. |

#### Tips

- Because nothing pulls in, this spin stays slow — that is the point. If you can stay centred and balanced here, your back spins have a foundation.

*The beginner back spin in this tutorial is the book's back open spin: the open position, held. Nobody titles this spin "back open" — the match is by technique, and Coach Mary's separate Part 2 (on the Back Scratch card below) is where the pull-in is added.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Weight balanced just behind the toe of the skating foot. Arms out to the side, open position — and the position is **held until balance and control are secure** before anything else happens. |
| **Feet** | Entry 1 (through the forward open position): from back crossovers, step into the circle on the left forward outside as for the forward open spin and hold the open position; then the right (free) foot moves **across in front** of the left foot and the left foot pushes off onto the toe of the right foot, onto a **right back outside edge**. The right leg then stretches out to the side. |
| **Feet (advanced entry)** | Turn a **right forward inside three**, left (free) foot behind the right (skating) foot; when the three completes, the **body turns in toward the foot** — not the foot toward the body. Better balance and control. |
| **Blade** | Right back outside edge throughout the spin. |
| **Exit** | The left (free) foot lowers beside the toe of the spinning foot, pulls back to the toe to slow the spin, then steps forward onto a **left forward outside edge**, held with the free foot extended behind over the print and arms out to the sides. |

#### Tips

- Don't rush the change of foot: the open position must be secure on the left before you push onto the right, or the back spin starts off-centre.

## Forward Scratch Spin {#forward-scratch-spin} <Badge type="warning" text="Intermediate" />

Like the one-footed spin — but after a turn or more, the free foot **crosses over** the skating foot and comes to rest outside the skating ankle. The tight, aerodynamic position, plus the arms pulling in, makes a very fast spin: the standard finish for all other spins.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | As the free foot draws in, the arms come together in a circle in front of the body — as though holding a **very large pumpkin** — elbows up. Then the hands clasp in the centre front, pull tightly in toward the body (bending at the elbows), and **press down with the palms** until the elbows are nearly straight. Least resistance = greatest speed. |
| **Feet** | Entry as for the one-footed spin. The right (free) leg swings back → front at skating-hip height; the free foot comes in to rest **to the outside, just above the skating knee**, free knee turned out. Then it slowly slides **down the outside of the skating leg** — heel pressing down, toe lifting — until the feet rest **crossed at the ankles**. |
| **Blade** | The three hooks the spin on a **back inside edge**, balance forward, first tooth of the toe pick, skating knee straightening as the free leg swings around. |
| **Exit** | The free leg lifts slightly to uncross at the ankle, steps down on the right foot, and pushes off onto a **right back outside edge**. Hold the finish. |

#### Tips

- Practise the crossed-ankle position, and the free-foot lift for the push-off, **at the barrier**.
- The press-down of the free foot and the clasp-and-press of the hands happen **together** — that simultaneity is where the speed comes from.
- **Variation — statue spin:** lift the arms up over the head instead of pressing them down.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Start from a secure back open position, arms out to the side, weight over the skating foot. The pull-in mirrors the forward scratch: arms circle in front like holding a large pumpkin, elbows up; hands clasp centre-front, pull in tight, then press down with the palms until the elbows are nearly straight. |
| **Feet** | The free (left) leg starts out to the side; the **body turns in the direction of the foot while the foot stays in place**, bringing the foot in front of the body — better balance and control. After several rotations the free foot draws straight in to rest **to the outside, just above the skating knee**, free knee turned out; then it slides down the outside of the skating leg, heel pressing down and toe lifting, to rest **crossed at the ankle** beside the skating foot. |
| **Blade** | Right back outside edge. The toe pick should **drag backward in a small circle** as you spin — if your weight tips forward and the pick pushes forward, the spin travels. |
| **Entries** | Three options: **(1)** either back open entry, shortened to a left forward outside three pushing onto the right back outside; **(2)** the right forward inside three entry — tighten the curve of the back outside edge as the three completes; **(3)** from a right back inside **pivot**, toe in the ice, shifting the weight completely onto the right back outside edge. |
| **Exit** | Press the skating toe pick forward to **stop the rotation**; the crossed free foot lifts **up and over** the skating foot (free knee well bent), the free leg **snaps back** into an extended position on the right back outside edge, arms breaking out to the finish — hands together centre-front as the leg uncrosses. |

#### Tips

- Use the exit as a jump-landing drill: the uncross-and-snap-back is exactly how you check out of a jump. Practise it slowly, then at the barrier, building speed and timing by repetition.
- Hold the back open position first, every time — a back scratch pulled in from an insecure open position never centres.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | The weight shift is the skill: as the second foot comes down, the pivotal axis must change and stay **perfectly centred**. |
| **Feet — European** | Begins from a **left forward open spin**; the right foot drops behind, passing over and behind the left heel with **no hesitation**. |
| **Feet — American** | Begins from a **right back open spin**; the left foot is placed on the ice **in front of and beside** the right foot, toe-to-toe. |
| **Feet — Two-footed** | Begins with the feet slightly **wider apart than usual**; the feet turn counter-clockwise, twisting the right foot behind the left. |
| **Blade** | The spin is performed on the **right back outside edge**, with the weight shifted to the **outside of the left foot** to change the pivotal axis and remain centred. |
| **Exit** | For all three types: the left foot lifts **up and over** the right foot, and the left leg **snaps back** into an extended position, remaining on the right back outside edge. |

#### Tips

- Enter from the open spin named for each type — the cross foot is a change of axis, not a new entry.
- No hesitation as the crossing foot comes down, or the spin tips off its centre.

## All seven upright spins at a glance

| Spin | Feet | Spinning edge | Free foot in the spin | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-Footed | Both | Flat, splitting to LBI ball / RFI heel | — (both feet down) | The first spin; where the pull-in is learned |
| One-Footed | One (right, as the book prints it) | Back inside | Rests beside the skating knee | The introductory one-foot spin |
| Forward Open | One (left) | Back inside | Stretched out to the side, never pulled in | Balance and control exercise; back-spin preparation |
| Back Open | One (right) | Back outside | Stretched out to the side | Mirror of the forward open; back-scratch preparation |
| Forward Scratch | One (left) | Back inside | Crosses over, slides down to the skating ankle | Very fast; the standard finish for other spins |
| Back Scratch | One (right) | Back outside | Drawn in front, crossed at the ankle | The fastest basic spin; the jump air position |
| Cross Foot | Both, crossed | Right back outside (axis shifted to the outside of the left foot) | — (both feet down, crossed) | European · American · two-footed entries |
