# Figure Skating Stops — Cheat Sheet

*From Chapter 4 of* The Complete Book of Figure Skating *(Carole Shulman, pp. 42–45). Four stops, in learning order. Golden rule from the book: never stop by running into the boards or dragging your toe pick — always brake with the blade.*

---

## 1. Snowplow Stop — the beginner stop (Fig. 4.1)

![Feet & blades, from above](stop1-snowplow-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![Snowplow stop](fig1-snowplow.png)

- **Body posture:** Upright torso, arms extended straight out to the sides for balance (as in the photo).
- **Foot posture:** Start with feet parallel, about shoulder-width apart. Press both feet into the ice and push/slide them out to the sides, finishing in a **V position** — the book's image is the plow on the front of a snow vehicle, whose point faces forward: seen from above, the **toes turn in toward each other and the heels push out**, forming an upside-down V (∧).
- **Blade used:** Both blades. Braking comes from pressing down so the blades skid and shave the ice.
- **Tips:**
  - The most basic stop — and the bridge to learning all the others. Advanced skaters rarely use it once harder stops are mastered.
  - Pressure check: done right, you'll shave a little snow off the ice and leave a visible skid mark.
  - Drill for beginners ("Yikes! Stripes!"): practice on a painted hockey line so the skid prints show clearly; or hold your balance over one foot while the other pushes out, one foot at a time.

## 2. T-Stop — the graceful, controlled stop (Fig. 4.2)

![Feet & blades, from above](stop2-tstop-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![T-stop](fig2-tstop.png)

- **Body posture:** Both arms stretched out to the sides, palms facing the ice and pressing down slightly. Ribcage lifted slightly so the back stays straight — this gives posture, balance, and stability.
- **Foot posture:** Glide forward on one foot. Lift the other foot and set it down **12–18 inches behind** the gliding foot, turned perpendicular. The back foot then drags/pulls lightly toward the front foot. You finish in an **upside-down T**: front foot pointing forward, back foot sideways behind it, heel of the front foot at the instep of the back foot. During the drag: front knee slightly bent, back knee straight; at the very end, both knees straighten.
- **Blade used:** The **back foot does the braking** (its blade skids with snowplow-like pressure). Progression: place the back foot on its **outside edge**, close behind the front foot — this builds the outside-edge control you'll need for the one-foot stop.
- **Tips:**
  - NOT a quick stop — it's a prepared stop: controlled and aesthetically pleasing.
  - Practice with each foot in front (right-front and left-front).
  - Barrier drill: face the boards, hold on with both hands, front toe perpendicular to the boards, and repeatedly draw the back foot up to the front to make "snow piles." This teaches exactly how much pressure stops you; as you improve, apply more pressure in less time.

## 3. Hockey Stop — the fast emergency stop (Fig. 4.3)

![Feet & blades, from above](stop3-hockey-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![Hockey stop](fig3-hockey.png)

- **Body posture:** Hips and shoulders stay **square** to your line of travel, shoulders held in a **checked** (counter-held) position. Arms remain straight out to the sides for the entire stop. Weight balanced slightly **in front of the center of the blade**, so the blades can swing sideways.
- **Foot posture:** Glide forward on two feet with knees bent. Rise up slightly (knees un-bend), then turn **both feet together to the side** — at a full stop the feet sit at a **90° angle to the body**. As the feet turn, bend the knees again **rapidly** to drive force into the ice.
- **Blade used:** Both blades, turned sideways so the edges bite and spray snow — this is what stops you fast.
- **Tips:**
  - The most advanced stop and the one used most by both hockey and figure skaters. This is your emergency brake — use it to avoid a person or object when there's no time for anything else.
  - Practice turning to **both** the right and the left.
  - Feel drill: twist in place from right to left, feet always moving together, finishing with feet pointing opposite to your shoulders and hips.

## 4. One-Footed Stop — the showy stop (Fig. 4.4)

![Feet & blades, from above](stop4-onefoot-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![One-footed stop](fig4-onefoot.png)

- **Body posture:** Identical to the hockey stop — same shoulder position (square, checked) and same arms straight out to the sides.
- **Foot posture:** A hockey stop on **one foot only**, turning to the right or the left. Lift either the front or the back foot; the foot that stays down turns sideways exactly as in a hockey stop. The free leg extends behind you (as in the photo).
- **Blade used:** One blade only — the supporting foot, using the same edge action as the hockey stop. Requires solid control of **both outside and inside edges** first.
- **Tips:**
  - Purely for show — never use it when the stop is urgent.
  - Learn the two-footed hockey stop first; the one-foot version is the same technique with a harder balance shift (the same shift as going from a two-foot glide to a one-foot glide).
  - Attempt it only after mastering outside and inside edges.

## Bonus: One-Foot Snowplow Stop — *not in the book*

▶ **Watch:** [How to stop on Ice Skates, Forward Snowplow Stop — Coach Julia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rhQY1_bTjVs) *(the one-foot version is the second half)*

![Feet & blades, from above](stop5-onefoot-snowplow-feet.svg)

*The snowplow done with one foot — a standard beginner progression (per Learn to Skate–style curricula and coaching sources such as LiveAbout and Edge Up Ice), included here because it bridges the two-foot snowplow and the T-stop.*

- **Body posture:** Same snowplow posture — upright torso, arms out to the sides, knees bent. Most of your weight sits over the straight gliding foot; the braking foot starts light.
- **Foot posture:** Glide forward on one foot, pointing straight ahead. Set the other foot down slightly ahead, turned **in** about **45°** — toe in, heel pushed out, like one half of the snowplow wedge — and ease it into a scrape.
- **Blade used:** The braking foot skids on the **inside part of its inside edge**, shaving snow; keep the blade flatter so it scrapes instead of catching. The gliding blade stays flat and straight.
- **Tips:**
  - The classic bridge from the two-foot snowplow to the T-stop and the hockey stop.
  - Start at the boards: holding on, push one foot out at a time to feel the scrape and build a little snow pile.
  - Add pressure gradually — digging in hard too fast makes the edge catch.
  - Practice braking with each foot; most beginners favor one side.

## Quick comparison

| Stop | Feet | Braking blade(s) | Body key cue | Speed of stop | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snowplow | Parallel → wedge (toes in, heels out) | Both, pressing/skidding out | Arms out, upright | Slow–medium | Beginner |
| T-Stop | Upside-down T | Back foot drag (later: outside edge) | Arms out, palms down, ribcage lifted | Slow, graceful | Beginner–intermediate |
| Hockey stop | Both turn 90° sideways | Both edges bite together | Shoulders square & checked, fast knee bend | Fastest — emergency | Advanced |
| One-footed | One foot sideways, other lifted | Single supporting blade | Same as hockey stop | Slow — for show | Advanced |
| One-foot snowplow (bonus, not in book) | One straight, one turned in ~45° | Inside edge of the braking foot | Weight on gliding foot, knees bent | Slow–medium | Beginner |
