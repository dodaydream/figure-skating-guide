# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

How you actually move on the ice — the pushes and glides that connect everything else, from the "Stroking Skills" section of *The Complete Book of Figure Skating*, Chapter 4.

::: warning The balance rule
Forward skating: **weight slightly back of centre** on the blade. Backward skating: **weight slightly forward of centre**. Nearly everything on this page hangs on that.
:::

::: tip Terminology
**Sculling = swizzle.** The book (2001) calls this pump step "sculling"; most rinks today call it a swizzle.
:::

## Which skill when?

| When | What |
|------|------|
| Move without lifting your feet | [Forward Sculling (Swizzle)](#forward-sculling-swizzle) |
| Your first real strokes | [Forward Glides](#forward-glides-basic-stroking) |
| More power and polish | [Advanced Forward Stroking](#advanced-forward-stroking) |
| Fastest acceleration | [Power Stroking](#power-stroking) |
| Start moving backward | [Backward Sculling (Swizzle)](#backward-sculling-swizzle) |
| First taste of moving backward | [The Snake (bonus)](#the-snake-backward-wiggle) |
| Glide backward | [Backward Glides](#backward-glides) & [Advanced Backward Skating](#advanced-backward-skating) |

Reading the diagrams: in every one you're looking down at your own feet. The red arrow points in the direction of travel — for backward skills it points down the page, toward you. Red dots mark the pressing edge; the pale blue curve in the sculling diagrams is the lemon-shaped track the feet trace; a dashed boot is a foot lifted off the ice.

| Tag | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| Body | Arms extended out to the sides for balance, knees bent; weight slightly back of centre on the blades — the balance for all forward skating. |
| Feet | Start with feet together in a V, toes pointed out. Push the feet forward and out to the sides until they're parallel, slightly more than shoulder-width apart; then straighten the knees and pull the feet back together, toes pointing in, until side by side. Repeat — out, then in — until it flows. (The diagram shows the start position at the bottom of the lemon-shaped track.) |
| Blade | The inside edge of each foot presses into the ice to drive the push out. |

#### Tips

- Neither foot ever leaves the ice — it's a pump step, and it works forward or backward.
- Be patient: finding just the right amount of pressure turns it into a graceful, effortless glide.

| Tag | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| Body | Balanced over both feet for the two-foot glide, weight slightly back of centre. Start near the boards until confidence grows and the barrier is no longer needed. |
| Feet | Begin parallel, about shoulder-width apart. Bend both knees; one foot (the book uses the right) rotates out to nearly 45° and presses against the ice, pushing with the entire blade; your weight transfers to the other foot, which glides forward on one foot. Bring the feet back together and repeat on the other side. |
| Blade | The pushing foot drives with the whole blade; the gliding foot runs flat and straight. |

#### Tips

- Aim to glide a distance of about three times your height on one foot.
- Repeating the push on the same foot is the one-footed pump step; alternate feet to travel across the ice.
- Practice until the push-and-glide becomes one smooth, continuous motion.

| Tag | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| Body | Upper body straight, back erect. A deeper knee and ankle bend before each stroke lengthens it — that bend is where the extra power and speed come from. |
| Feet | Begin with feet together. Ankle and knee bend forward; the push-off leg straightens and presses the ice, propelling the skating foot forward. The push-off foot then leaves the ice and stretches fully behind the skating foot, knee turned out, toe pointed. Finally the free leg lowers beside the skating foot, both knees bent — and the stroke repeats on the other side. |
| Blade | Push from the inside edge — **never the toe pick**. The middle to the ball of the foot presses on the blade. |

#### Tips

- The whole stroke in three words: **bend, extension, stretch**.
- **Strength drill (wall sits):** back against a wall, feet 12–18 inches out, toes forward; slide down slowly until your thighs are perpendicular to the wall; hold 30 seconds. Best done supervised.

| Tag | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| Body | Knees bent, chest up, arms out to the sides. Balance on the balls of your feet — the backward-skating balance — and let your hips sway side to side, against your shoulders. |
| Feet | Feet together and parallel, both staying on the ice the whole time. Shift your weight from one foot to the other, pressing out a little with each foot in turn; each small press nudges you backward, and your path weaves like a snake. |
| Blade | Both blades stay down, weight over the balls of the feet; each foot presses out in turn as the weight lands on it. |

#### Tips

- The book slips this into a Teaching Tip under Backward Glides: tots pretend to be bunnies wiggling through the carrot patch; adults are told to think "hula hoop."
- In the Taiwan association's beginner progression it's called the snake (蛇行), the step right after the backward duck walk.
- Glance over your shoulder before you start — you're travelling where you can't see.
- Wiggling but going nowhere? Walk backward slowly with your toes turned in to feel the momentum, then go back to wiggling.

## All seven skills at a glance

| Skill | Feet pattern | Weight on blade | Power source | Direction |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|--------------|-----------|
| Forward sculling (swizzle) | V → wide → together, repeat | Slightly back of centre | Inside-edge push out, pull in | Forward |
| Forward glides / stroking | Push at ~45°, glide on the other foot | Slightly back of centre | Whole-blade push | Forward |
| Advanced forward stroking | Together → push → free leg stretched behind | Slightly back of centre | Inside-edge push, deep bend | Forward |
| Power stroking | Same strokes, explosive | Slightly back of centre | Knee-and-ankle snap | Forward |
| Backward sculling (swizzle) | Toes-in start → back & out → together | Slightly forward of centre | Inside-edge push | Backward |
| Backward glides | Duck walk → two-foot → one-foot glide | Forward, over the ball of the foot | Short steps, then coast | Backward |
| Advanced backward skating | Push across, free foot hovers | Forward | Alternating backward strokes | Backward |
| Snake / backward wiggle (bonus) | Feet together, weight shifts side to side | Slightly forward, over the balls of the feet | Side-to-side presses + hip sway | Backward |
