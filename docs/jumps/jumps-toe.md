# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

The toe-assisted family of Chapter 8 of *The Complete Book of Figure Skating*. Every jump on this page takes off backwards, and every one of them is launched with help from a toe pick planted in the ice — what tells them apart is **which edge the skating foot is on** and **which toe does the picking**. Two of them are half-rotation position jumps (split and stag); the other four are full-revolution jumps.

::: tip What makes a jump a toe jump
Toe jumps are those that **use the vaulting action of the toe to assist in the takeoff**. Like a pole-vaulter's pole, the toe helps to create lift — which can result in a very dynamic jump. Contrast the [Edge Jumps](./jumps-edge.md), where there is no interruption on the takeoff and the jump leaves straight from the edge.
:::

::: info Which rotation are these pages written for?
All descriptions in the book — and in all six Jumps files — assume a skater rotating **left / counter-clockwise**. Skaters who rotate to the right reverse the starting foot and the direction throughout.
:::

## Which jump when?

- [A split position in the air, only half a turn → Split Jump](#split-jump)
- [The split jump with the front leg bent under you → Stag Jump](#stag-jump)
- [Your first full-revolution toe jump — and the usual second jump in a combination → Toe Loop](#toe-loop)
- [Toe loop mechanics, but taking off from a back *inside* edge → Toe Walley](#toe-walley)
- [Back inside edge plus a toe pick, landing on the picking foot → Flip](#flip)
- [Back *outside* edge plus a toe pick — the flip's harder twin → Lutz](#lutz)

How to read the prints: Figures 8.21 and 8.23 are the prints a correct takeoff leaves on the ice — the dashed line is the line of travel, the solid curves are the edges skated, the dot is where the toe pick goes in, and the labelled curve after the dot is the landing edge. The takeoff edge in the print is the ground truth for each jump: **back inside for the flip, back outside for the Lutz**.

## Split Jump {#split-jump} <Badge type="tip" text="Beginner–Intermediate" />

A position jump, not a rotation jump: **half a rotation** in the air with the legs in a full split. Take off from a **left back inside edge**, placing the **right toe** into the ice; land on the **left toe**, then step onto a **right forward inside edge**. It must be entered and executed **with speed**.

*No dedicated single-technique video verified for this jump yet.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Arms are left **out to the side** for the beginning skater; with skill and practice the arm positions may be varied. In the **Russian split** variation the body does not turn to the front in the air but faces to the side, with each hand reaching toward a foot — when done well, a spectacular jump that demonstrates great agility. |
| **Feet** | From the back edge, the right toe is placed into the ice and the feet are drawn together, bringing the **left foot through and forward** into the air in a split position. The left leg (in front) should be **turned out** and the right leg (behind) **turned in** — the same placement that would be used to do the splits on the floor. **Both toes pointed.** The takeoff and landing are a **scissor action** that begins with the left toe and ends on the left toe, before stepping onto the right forward inside edge. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff from the left back inside edge with the right toe pick planted. A great deal of **knee bend** is required on the takeoff to get the elevation and the timing for the split to occur. As with all forward inside edge landings, it is common to follow through with a **forward inside three turn** to a back outside edge. |

#### Tips

- **Flexibility is the main factor.** Practise the splits on the floor.
- Stand at the barrier and practise the feel of the split in the air, using the support of your arms on the boards to delay the quick action of the jump that takes place on the ice.

## Stag Jump {#stag-jump} <Badge type="tip" text="Beginner–Intermediate" />

The split jump with one change: the **front leg is bent at the knee and brought under the body** in a stag position. Same takeoff — **left back inside edge**, **right toe** placed in the ice — and the same half rotation, with the legs parallel to the ice; land on the **left toe**, followed by a **right forward inside edge**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | The takeoff is the same as for the split jump; the only change is the body's position in the air. Arm positions can be varied depending on skill and creativity. |
| **Feet** | From the back edge, the right toe is placed into the ice and the feet are drawn together, bringing the **left foot forward in a bent-knee position, up and under the body**. The **right leg remains extended behind** the body in a straight, horizontal position. |
| **Blade** | Same as the split jump throughout — left back inside takeoff edge, right toe pick, and the landing is the same as for the split jump: left toe, then the right forward inside edge. |

#### Tips

- Practise the stag position by **jumping up in place** and hitting the correct position in the air before landing.
- Stand at the boards and practise the feel of the stag while using the support of your arms on the boards to delay the quick action of the jump on the ice. You may need to use a **spotter**.

## Toe Loop {#toe-loop} <Badge type="tip" text="Beginner–Intermediate" />

The first full-revolution toe jump in the book's teaching order for singles (Salchow, then toe loop). It takes off from a **right back outside edge**, assisted by a **toe tap from the left foot**, makes **one revolution** in the air, and lands on a **right back outside edge**.

[▶ Watch: Toe Loop Jump Tutorial for Figure Skaters — Coach Mary Figure Skating (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2N1L5vYyY_0)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | As the left toe goes into the ice, the **left arm extends forward** in front of the body and the **right arm back**. The momentum of the legs and arms vaults the body into the air for one full revolution. The arms **pull in and across the chest**, then open out for the landing. |
| **Feet** | Most common entry: a **right forward inside three** — the back outside edge of the turn is held, and the left toe is placed into the ice for the takeoff. (Another entry is from a left forward outside drop three, the left toe placed into the ice after the turn.) On the takeoff the **right foot pulls toward the left toe**; the right knee bends in preparation and continues to **draw past the left toe**. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff edge and landing edge are the same: **right back outside**. Only the left toe pick touches — it assists, it does not become the takeoff foot. In the book's progression grouping the toe loop is a jump that **changes feet**: the pick goes in with the left foot, the landing is on the right. |

#### Tips

- Practise the pulling action of the takeoff at the barrier — place the left toe in the ice and draw the right foot toward it.
- **Watch the placement of the picking toe:** placed too far to the side, it will cause a pre-rotated jump.
- Because it takes off from the back outside landing edge every jump lands on, the toe loop is the natural add-on jump — see [Combination Jumps](./jumps-combinations.md).

## Toe Walley {#toe-walley} <Badge type="warning" text="Intermediate" />

A toe loop from the other edge. A toe Walley is executed from a **right back inside edge** by placing the **left toe** in the ice, lifting off, and rotating **one full turn** in the air before landing on a **right back outside edge**. The difference between a toe loop and a toe Walley is exactly that takeoff edge: toe loop from a **back outside** edge, toe Walley from a **back inside** edge.

![Book photo: a skater's legs and blades at the toe Walley takeoff — the left toe placed into the ice, the jump taking off from a right back inside edge](/jumps-toe-assets/fig820-toe-walley-takeoff.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.20: takeoff position in the toe Walley*

*No dedicated single-technique video verified for this jump yet.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | The arms start in a **strong checked position** — left arm in front of the body, right arm behind. The left arm continues to extend forward and the right arm back through the takeoff; the arms pull in and across the chest for the revolution, then open out for the landing. |
| **Feet** | Preparation: a **left forward outside three**, followed by a step down onto the **right back inside edge**. The left toe is placed in the ice for the takeoff, and as it goes in, the **right foot pulls toward the left toe**; the right knee bends in preparation and continues to draw **past the left toe**, using the momentum of the leg action and arms to project the body into the air. |
| **Blade** | The **right (skating) knee is bent** on the preparation, and the skating foot stays on the **back inside edge** all the way to takeoff — that inside edge is the whole identity of the jump. Landing is on a right back outside edge. |

#### Tips

- Practise jumping from the right back inside edge to the left back inside edge **without rotation**, to feel the action of the takeoff.
- **Remain on the back inside edge at all times** during that drill — if the edge rolls outside, you are rehearsing a toe loop instead.

## Flip {#flip} <Badge type="warning" text="Intermediate" />

One full turn from a **left back inside edge**: place the **right toe** into the ice to lift off, rotate **one full turn**, and land on a **right back outside edge** — the picking foot, so in the book's grouping the flip is a **same-foot** takeoff and landing jump.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | In a well-executed flip the back is **straight but slightly inclined forward**, with the shoulders level; the **left arm is in front** of the body over the print and the **right arm behind**. At takeoff both arms **pull down and then up and in** toward the body, and stay **close to the body at chest level** for the revolution. In the air the left hip is slightly lifted and the body weight stays over the right side; before landing, the left knee lifts slightly out and the arms open, pushing back to a **checked landing position**. |
| **Feet** | Preparation is either a **left forward outside three** or a **right forward inside Mohawk** — and the turn must **not swing wide**, but be performed on more of a straight line. The right (free) leg **reaches low and straight behind** the body, with the body weight over the left (skating) foot. On the takeoff the **left foot draws toward the right**, transferring the weight over the right foot. In the air the **right leg is straight** and the **left leg crossed slightly over in front** of the right. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff from the **left back inside edge** — read it in Figure 8.21: three turn, toe pick in (the dot), land on the **right back outside** curve. |

#### Tips

- Standing on two feet, jump up and turn one revolution, landing again on two feet. Draw the arms in on the rotation and out for the landing — and keep a **spotter** close by until your balance is secure.

## Lutz {#lutz} <Badge type="danger" text="Intermediate–Advanced" />

Exactly like the flip — **except the takeoff edge**. The Lutz is executed by skating on a **left back outside edge**, placing the **right toe** into the ice, lifting off, and rotating **one full turn** before landing on a **right back outside edge**. That one edge is the entire difference between the two jumps, and holding it is what makes the Lutz the last single taught in the book's order (Salchow, toe loop, loop, flip, Lutz).

::: danger The classic error — the "flutz"
The most common Lutz error is **changing from a back outside edge to a back inside edge just before takeoff**. If this occurs, the jump becomes a flip instead of a Lutz — a cheated Lutz, commonly called a **"flutz."** Check your takeoff against the print in Figure 8.23: if the curve before the dot is an inside curve, it wasn't a Lutz.
:::

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Same frame as the flip: back **straight but slightly inclined forward**, shoulders level, **left arm in front** over the print and **right arm behind**; both arms pull down, then up and in toward the body. In the air the **right leg is straight** and the **left leg crossed slightly over in front** of the right, the left hip slightly lifted, body weight over the right side, arms **close to the body at chest level**. After one full revolution the left knee lifts slightly to uncross and the arms open before landing. |
| **Feet** | Most commonly entered from **back crossovers**, then **holding the left back outside edge** in preparation. The right (free) leg **reaches low and straight behind** the skater, body weight over the left foot. The left foot draws toward the right, with the weight kept over the right foot for the takeoff — and the jump lands on that same right foot: a **same-foot** takeoff and landing in the book's grouping. |
| **Blade** | **Left back outside, held to the moment of takeoff.** The toe pick goes in while the edge is still outside (Figure 8.23: LBO curve → dot → RBO landing curve). The instant the edge changes to inside before the pick, the jump stops being a Lutz — see the callout above. Landing is on the **right back outside edge**. |

#### Tips

- The book's edge-holding exercise: do back crossovers, hold the left back outside edge for the takeoff, place the right toe into the ice, and **jump straight up without rotating** — purely to practise remaining on the outside edge without changing over to the inside edge.
- Read your own print afterwards against Figure 8.23. The print does not lie about the edge you took off from.

## All six toe jumps at a glance

| Jump | Rotation | Takeoff edge | Toe pick | Landing | The thing to watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Split | ½ | Left back inside | Right toe | Left toe → right forward inside | Speed in, flexibility for the split; scissor action |
| Stag | ½ | Left back inside | Right toe | Left toe → right forward inside | Front leg bent up and under the body; back leg straight and horizontal |
| Toe loop | 1 | **Right back outside** | Left toe | Right back outside | Picking toe placed too far to the side → pre-rotated jump |
| Toe Walley | 1 | **Right back inside** | Left toe | Right back outside | Same mechanics as the toe loop — the inside takeoff edge is the difference |
| Flip | 1 | **Left back inside** | Right toe | Right back outside (the picking foot) | Entry turn must not swing wide; keep it on a straight line |
| Lutz | 1 | **Left back outside** | Right toe | Right back outside (the picking foot) | Changing to an inside edge before takeoff turns it into a flip — a "flutz" |
