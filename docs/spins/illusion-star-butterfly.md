# Illusion, Star & Butterfly

Three spectacular elements built on spinning principles. Because they share a few common points, the book teaches them **in a progressive format** — illusion, then star, then the butterfly's exercise ladder — and only once good spin technique is mastered. Mastering the illusion, the star, and the off-ice butterfly exercises is **mandatory** before attempting the butterfly on ice. This section of the book was provided by **Ann-Margareth Frei-Hall** — three-time Swedish champion and World and Olympic competitor — and edited by Carole Shulman.

Three elements:

- [Illusion](#illusion) — a one-footed pinwheel, like a cartwheel on one foot
- [Star](#star) — a forward outside three executed in a high spiral position
- [Butterfly](#butterfly) — an aerial cartwheel landing in a back camel spin

## Illusion

_The illusion appears to be like a one-footed cartwheel. The body weight remains on one foot while the free foot swings around in a pinwheel action and, simultaneously, the body alternates between a forward position bent toward the ice and an upright position over the skating foot._

![Illusion (Fig. 7.20): (a) after a small forward kick the free foot swings down, then (b) backward in a pendulum motion; (c) the finish position](/spins-assets/illusion/fig720-illusion.png)

*From the book — Figure 7.20: illusion.*

_No dedicated illusion tutorial exists on the preferred channels, so this card follows the disclosed-line practice used elsewhere in the guide._

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | On the back kick, the upper body goes **forward and down** on the right (skating) knee as the left (free) leg lifts high behind the body. When the free leg reaches its highest point, **both legs must be very straight** — the knees must not be bent at this time — so maximum extension can take place. The hand on the same side as the skating leg can reach for the ice to help the body bend forward. As confidence increases, that same arm swings in a circular motion: pointing **straight up** when the ankles are together in the upright position, then forward and down toward the ice during the back kick. *(Some skaters prefer using the opposite arm.)* |
| **Feet** | After the opening three, kick the left (free) leg **straight forward** — be careful the foot is not kicked around in a circle. The free leg then comes down fast, passing **very close to the right (skating) ankle**, and kicks **backward, up, and around** in a pendulum motion in the direction of the spin — push powerfully, to keep the rotation going. A second forward kick, following the second three, is aimed in exactly the same direction as the first. |
| **Blade** | Begin with a **right back outside three**; the forward kick happens during the short **right forward inside edge** that follows. A second three — a **right forward inside three** — carries the swing-through, and the pendulum back kick lands on the **right back outside edge** after it. Exit on a right back outside edge. |

**Tips**

- **Barrier drill:** stand sideways next to the boards, hold on with one hand, and practise the opposing action of the free leg and the body position — with a **spotter** at your side, and nobody standing directly in front of or behind you.
- Use the **toe picks** to catch the momentum of the action, so you don't slip off the front or back of the skate.

## Star

_Think of the star as a forward outside three executed in a spiral position, with the head, both hands, and both feet as the five points of the star. The book gives no figure for the star — for the position itself, see the spiral photos on the [Forward Spiral](/freestyle-moves.md#forward-spiral) card in Chapter 5._

_No dedicated star tutorial exists on the preferred channels — today the element survives mainly inside spiral sequences — so this card follows the disclosed-line practice used elsewhere in the guide._

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | Totally **horizontal** in a natural spiral position, and lowered so the free leg can lift high behind. The back must **not arch** during the kick — arching would be a tremendous, counterproductive strain. Arms stay **outstretched to the sides** during the star. |
| **Feet** | The right (free) leg rises behind the body in a high spiral position **immediately** as it leaves the ice, reaching the **highest point in the middle of the three** — the skating foot may even lift off the ice if the spiral kick is strong and timed after the middle of the three. The leg is kicked **straight up**; it only looks as though it goes around, because the body is turning as the leg lifts. As soon as the three is executed in the spiral position, the right (free) leg comes down behind the left (skating) foot and its toe presses into the ice to push into the next star. Both knees are **very straight** during the kicked spiral position. |
| **Blade** | A **left forward outside three**. Before it, both knees bend and the body leans forward; the toe of the right (free) leg pushes into the turn. |

**Tips**

- **Timing is everything:** it is not possible to do a star if the free leg is slow and only reaches its highest point *after* the turn — it must peak in the middle of the three.
- Don't straighten the body after each star: keeping a firm, forward position allows faster turns with fewer adjustments. Build entry speed with several fast **chaines** (forward outside threes with a toe push) — especially if the body is already leaning forward at the end of the second chaine. A variation ends a star sequence with a couple of fast chaines with the upper body upright.
- **Learn it at the barrier:** stand facing away from the barrier toward the centre of the rink, knees well bent; turn and hold the barrier with both hands as the free leg lifts up behind the body into the spiral position, both legs straightened, body leaning forward with the back **flat, not arched** — in slow motion, the free leg at its highest point in the middle of the three.

## Butterfly

_Butterflies can easily be learned after a flying camel spin is mastered. A butterfly is an aerial cartwheel in which the body position in the air is horizontal to the ice, as in a flying camel — but with an explosive arch of the back as the skater leaves the ice._

![Butterfly (Fig. 7.21): (a) takeoff, (b) air position, and (c) landing](/spins-assets/illusion/fig721-butterfly.png)

*From the book — Figure 7.21: butterfly: (a) takeoff, (b) air position, and (c) landing.*

_No dedicated butterfly tutorial exists on the preferred channels, so this card follows the disclosed-line practice used elsewhere in the guide._

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | Horizontal to the ice in the air, arms **stretched out to the side and stable**. The rotation comes from throwing the upper body **forcefully from right to left** in a circular motion; the right arm must stay behind until the last second. Because of the explosive arch at takeoff, the back must be strong — no sagging on the landing. |
| **Feet** | The difference from a flying camel is the takeoff leg action: for a flying camel the free leg reaches up to the side and slightly forward into a spread-eagled air position, but for a butterfly the **free (right) leg is kicked straight backward** during the takeoff and comes down as the other leg goes up in a **scissor action**. The power of that kick determines the timing and height — the jump must occur at the exact moment the leg goes up. The landing is in a **back camel spin position**, on the right toe. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff from the **toe** — the takeoff will be from the toes with no slipping or sliding. Although the feet are in fact parallel at takeoff, they feel turned in (pigeon-toed), because they did not change position while the body rotated from right to left during the barrel spin. The full pattern traces a **reverse-$ sign** on the ice: two half circles, each with a radius of about five feet, with a straight long axis running through them; the barrel spin turns on the axis before the jump. The body should remain **low and parallel to the ice** through the landing. |

**The exercise ladder — learn in this order, off the ice first**

1. **Backward French can-can** — feet side by side, knees slightly bent; kick first the right (landing) leg, then the left, backward, changing legs in midair, continuously.
2. **Single scissor action** — the same, but with only one change of foot. Always kick the landing leg backward first.
3. **Barrel position** — feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent as though sitting on a low stool or barrel, arms in a circle in front of the chest; open the arms firmly to the sides as you kick backward into one scissor change. This must be perfectly mastered before moving on.
4. **Spiral jump** — from the perfect barrel position, tip the body forward into a horizontal lean as the arms go to the sides and you scissor again; land in a back camel spin position, body leaning forward, left (free) leg extended, arms out. Once the body leans forward, it stays forward.
5. **Hair-raising drop** — stand up straight and lift both feet at the same time, then drop quickly back down; it requires such speed you'll feel your hair rising.
6. **Moth in the cocoon** *(not quite a butterfly yet, but close)* — from a slightly higher barrel position: bend both knees very fast (as in the hair-raising drop), lean forward, open the arms, kick back in a scissor action, and land in a back camel position. It should feel like a jumped spiral — the first right (free) leg kick in the air gives the jump its correct timing and height.
7. **Body twist** *(very advanced)* — start in the barrel position with the upper body turned tightly to the right, most weight on the right leg; as you lean forward, unwind the body to the left, open the arms, and shift the weight onto the left toe with the feet pigeon-toed; finally add the jump, always kicking the right leg straight backward — never to the side. Don't worry about the twist and release action until everything else is mastered; add it later.

**On the ice**

Review the floor work in order: walk high up on the toe picks to feel the grip; can-can on ice; the barrel takeoff drill (weight shifts to the left foot, rock forward to the toe pick and jump, right leg kicked high and back, lean forward, arms open, land on the right toe in back camel); a **barrel spin** — at least five rotations on a circle about a foot across — to lend rotation; an eyes-closed simulation of the weight shift; the moth in the cocoon off a barrel spin of at least two turns; and the body twist. As proficiency increases the barrel spin gets shorter and shorter — half a turn at the end — with the body thrown quickly from one side of the axis line to the other. If the landing feels weak and tentative, do a few [flying camels](./flying-spins.md#flying-camel-spin) to recover the feeling of a strong landing.

**Tips**

- Progress slowly, mastering each step before moving on — and go back a few steps whenever there's confusion. Bad habits are much harder to fix than slow progress is to speed up.
- Several butterflies can be combined, one after the other, ending in a back camel spin.
