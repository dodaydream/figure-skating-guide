# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

Freestyle moves are everything between the jumps, spins, and footwork — the artistic moves that add interest and variety to a program. From Chapter 5 of *The Complete Book of Figure Skating*: spirals, lunges, shoot-the-ducks, pivots, spread eagles, and the Bauer.

::: tip The big idea
These are skills nearly every skater can learn, from tot to adult. They also feed the bigger elements: a shoot-the-duck builds the strength and balance for sit spins, a pivot can enter or exit a spin, and a lunge can dress up a spin or jump entrance. Artistry is available at every level — you don't need multi-revolution jumps to perform.
:::

## Which move when?

- [Your first showpiece glide → Forward Spiral](#forward-spiral)
- [The same glide, backwards → Back Spiral](#back-spiral)
- [Low, dramatic, and beginner-friendly → Lunge](#lunge)
- [Deep knee bend on one foot → Shoot-the-Duck](#shoot-the-duck)
- [Your first pivot — the simplest one → Forward Inside Pivot](#forward-inside-pivot)
- [The difficult, seldom-used pivot → Forward Outside Pivot](#forward-outside-pivot)
- [Pivot that exits into a two-foot spin → Back Inside Pivot](#back-inside-pivot)
- [The death-spiral pivot, fast and spectacular → Back Outside Pivot](#back-outside-pivot)
- [Heel-to-heel on inside edges → Inside Spread Eagle](#inside-spread-eagle)
- [Heel-to-heel on outside edges → Outside Spread Eagle](#outside-spread-eagle)
- [Spread eagle with a bent front knee → The Bauer](#the-bauer)

How to read the diagrams: sustained glides are shown as a **single diagram** looking down at your own feet, with a red arrow for the direction of travel. Pivots are staged moves around a planted toe, so each pivot is shown in three keyframes — **1 · Entry, 2 · The pivot, 3 · Exit** — with no travel arrow (the stage order shows the progression). Red dot = the edge doing the work, or the planted toe in a pivot; pale blue = the print left on the ice; a dashed boot is the free foot, lifted off the ice. Exceptions to watch for: in the **lunge** the trailing boot is solid because it drags on the ice (on the side of the boot, not the blade), and in the **spread eagles** and the **Bauer** both boots are solid and on the same print, heel-to-heel with the toes turned out in opposite directions.

## Forward Spiral <Badge type="tip" text="Foundation" /> {#forward-spiral}

In ballet, an arabesque. One of the most beautiful moves in skating when done well — it takes flexibility, balance, and control. Start from a forward two-foot glide once your one-foot glide is solid.

![Feet and blades from above: gliding forward on one foot along a gentle curve, the dashed free foot held behind, parallel to the skating foot](/freestyle-assets/spiral-forward-feet.svg)
*Feet & blades, from above — balanced over the back of the blade, free leg lifted behind.*

![Book photo: a skater in a high forward spiral, chest parallel to the ice, free leg extended up behind](/freestyle-assets/fig51-forward-spiral.png)
*From the book — Figure 5.1: the forward spiral*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Arms out to the side. Waist bends forward to **90°** from the legs, back **arched** — coach Tom Zakrajsek's "banana position": the curve from foot to head resembles a banana. Beginning goal: free leg parallel with the torso; with control, raise it higher while the chest stays parallel to the ice. |
| **Feet** | One skating foot gliding forward; the other leg lifts slowly behind, straight. Practise on **each foot**. No mirror? Check your reflection in the Plexiglas for straight legs and an arched back. |
| **Blade** | Balanced over the **back of the blade**. The more you lean forward, the more your weight pushes backward on the blade and the more your back arches. First in a straight line, then on **inside and outside edges**; advanced entries come from a crossover or a change of edge. |

#### Tips

- Flexibility is key, and off-ice stretching is part of the move: barre work, floor spreads, splits progressions.
- Never overstretch — stretch to the point of resistance, hold about 5–7 seconds, release. Never pump or push past that point.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Arms out to the sides for balance. Waist bends forward until the **torso is parallel to the ice**, back **arched**. The more you lean forward, the more weight pushes toward the front of the blade and the more the back arches. With control, raise the free leg higher and higher. |
| **Feet** | One skating foot gliding backward; the other leg lifts slowly behind. Practise on **both feet**. |
| **Blade** | Weight balances over the **front of the blade** — the opposite of the forward spiral. First in a straight line, then on **outside and inside edges**; entries from back crossovers, or advanced with a change of edge. |

#### Tips

- **Look around before you begin.** You're travelling backward, head turned away from the free-leg side — make sure the ice is clear and there's no chance of running into another skater.
- Use the same off-ice stretching as the forward spiral, plus back bends to build back strength.

## Lunge <Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> {#lunge}

Bend the skating knee fully, stretch the free leg behind, and draw it along the ice behind the skating foot. A stand-alone move, or a variation on the entrance or exit of a spin or jump. No book photo — the diagram is the reference.

![Feet and blades from above: the skating boot gliding straight ahead on a bent knee, the trailing boot behind it turned out and dragging on the side of the boot](/freestyle-assets/lunge-feet.svg)
*Feet & blades, from above — skating foot on its edge; trailing boot on its side, not its blade.*

</div>

[▶ Watch: Learn To Do a Forward Lunge on Ice, Basic Figure Skating Tutorial — Coach Julia (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FMWNmLUwrWU)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Head **up**, arms stretched out to the side, hips **square** — even as the free leg turns out to an open position. The skating knee bends to **fully bent**. To rise: lean the torso slightly more forward over the skating foot as the trailing foot lifts, and finish in a two-foot forward glide, arms still out. |
| **Feet** | From a forward two-foot glide: pick up one foot, turn that leg out, and place it behind — **the side of the boot goes on the ice, never the blade**: point the toes up and arch the foot so the blade can't catch. The free leg ends fully extended behind the torso, boot dragging on the ice. |
| **Blade** | The skating foot carries the glide; the trailing foot contributes **no edge** — it's boot-side on the ice. |

#### Tips

- Practise on **both feet**.
- Backward lunges exist too — use extreme caution placing the foot so the **boot, not the blade**, touches the ice.
- Build bend-and-rise strength at the boards, weight forward — only 2–3 reps per foot at first. Don't strain.

## Shoot-the-Duck <Badge type="tip" text="Beginner–Intermediate" /> {#shoot-the-duck}

Bend fully on the skating knee and stretch the free foot directly out in front, travelling in a straight line. This is the strength-and-balance builder behind the sit spin.

![Feet and blades from above: one boot gliding straight ahead, the dashed free boot stretched out in front of it, lifted off the ice](/freestyle-assets/shoot-the-duck-feet.svg)
*Feet & blades, from above — sunk fully on the skating knee, free foot straight out front.*

![Book photo: a young skater in a shoot-the-duck, sunk deep on one leg with the other stretched out in front](/freestyle-assets/fig52-shoot-the-duck.png)
*From the book — Figure 5.2: the shoot-the-duck*

[▶ Watch: How To Do A Shoot The Duck - In Figure Skates!! Ice Skating Tutorial — Coach Julia (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TENZbpWASjY)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Lower until the skating knee is **fully bent**. Arms kept **out in front** — they may hold the extended free foot or knee for balance; the advanced version keeps the arms out to the side and takes much more strength. To rise: lean the torso forward and rise slowly over the skating leg. |
| **Feet** | Skating foot under you, knee fully bent; free foot stretched **directly out in front**, off the ice. Develop it on **both feet, both edges, and both directions**. |
| **Blade** | Weight starts toward the **back of the blade**, then pushes **forward** as the body lowers to keep your centre of balance — and keeps pushing proportionately forward as you sink. |

#### Tips

- Learn it in order: a **dip** on a two-foot glide (both knees fully bent) → a forward one-foot glide → the shoot-the-duck, starting with the free foot already in front.

| **Feet** | Example, counterclockwise: skate slowly forward on a small curve on the **right inside edge**. #### Tips

- The book's training tip: don't try to turn on the pivot too early, and don't plant the toe too quickly or forcefully — **"it is a matter of touch."** Practise just placing the toe and controlling your weight over it.
- A mini-pivot makes a handy entrance into a two-foot spin.

## Inside Spread Eagle <Badge type="warning" text="Intermediate–Advanced" /> {#inside-spread-eagle}

In ballet, the second position. It depends entirely on the ability to open the hips — not every skater can do it — and done well it's a dramatic move.

![Feet and blades from above: two boots on the same curve, a leg-split apart, heel-to-heel with the toes turned out in opposite directions](/freestyle-assets/spread-inside-feet.svg)
*Feet & blades, from above — both boots on the one print, inside edges, toes opposed.*

![Book photo: a skater in an inside spread eagle, legs split heel-to-heel, one arm raised](/freestyle-assets/fig56-inside-spread-eagle.png)
*From the book — Figure 5.6: the inside spread eagle*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | **Both knees straight and locked, back arched, arms extended out to the sides.** Everything hinges on a genuinely **open hip position**. Once it's solid, experiment with creative arm positions. |
| **Feet** | Begin on the **left forward inside edge**; swing the **right (free) foot forward past** the left, then swing it back and place it on the ice **about 12–18 inches behind** the left foot, **on the same curve** — heel-to-heel, toes turned out. Practise leading with both right and left feet. |
| **Blade** | Leading foot on a **forward inside edge**, trailing foot on a **back inside edge** — inside edges throughout. |

#### Tips

- Build hip openness at the barrier: legs stretched in opposite directions, insteps braced against the barrier, hips pressed into it, lean back slightly with an arched back. The barrier's resistance helps stretch the hips open.

#### Master the Move — Paul Wylie

![Book photo: Paul Wylie performing an inside spread eagle, back strongly arched, arms extended](/freestyle-assets/wylie-spread-eagle.png)

- His signature move is classy because of impeccable technique — **strongly arched back, extended arms, overall carriage and expression**. He turned something ordinary into the extraordinary.
- His difficulty trick: linking **inside and outside spread eagles in a continuous serpentine pattern down the ice** — see the Outside Spread Eagle card for that drill.

## Outside Spread Eagle <Badge type="danger" text="Advanced" /> {#outside-spread-eagle}

![Book photo: a skater in an outside spread eagle, legs split heel-to-heel, arms extended to the sides](/freestyle-assets/fig57-outside-spread-eagle.png)

| **Body** | **Both knees straight and locked, back arched, arms extended out to the side.** Stroke forward first to gain speed. |
| **Feet** | Example: start on the **left forward outside edge**; swing the right (free) foot forward past the left, then back, and place it behind on the same curve, heel-to-heel and turned out — the inside spread eagle's motion, mirrored onto the other edge. Practise on **both feet**. |
| **Blade** | Both feet on **outside edges** — leading foot forward outside, trailing foot back outside. |

#### Tips

- Advanced entry, no swing: from **back crossovers**, hold the right back outside edge and rotate your body around so the left foot can be placed directly on the ice.
- Wylie's serpentine drill: alternate outside and inside spread eagles down the ice, **lifting your body slightly before each change of edge** to reduce friction as you change.

## The Bauer <Badge type="danger" text="Advanced" /> {#the-bauer}

Named for Ina Bauer, German champion from 1956 to 1960. A dramatic variation of the spread eagle: bend the front knee, keep the back knee straight.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Upper body **arched** — dramatically, it can be done in a **layback position with the arms overhead or out to the side**. It takes flexibility, muscular strength, and control: splits, laybacks, and quad/hamstring strength work like pliés and lunges. |
| **Feet** | Most easily entered from **back crossovers**. Example (right foot in front): hold the **left back inside edge** as the **right foot is placed in front** — front knee **bent**, back knee **straight**, feet in the spread-eagle turned-out line. |
| **Blade** | Front foot on a **forward outside edge**, back foot on a **back inside edge**. |

#### Tips

- Straight line or circle is a pressure question: **equalised balance and edge pressure** between the front and back legs travels in a **straight line** (usually corner-to-corner on the diagonal). Favour a strong outside edge and it circles; put both feet on forward inside edges and it circles the other way.
