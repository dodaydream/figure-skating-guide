# Figure Skating Edges & Crossovers — Cheat Sheet

*From the "Edges" and "Crossovers" sections of Chapter 4 of* The Complete Book of Figure Skating *(Carole Shulman, pp. 49–56). Edges are the foundation of figure skating — speed, balance, lean, and control all come from them. There are eight: outside/inside × right/left × forward/backward, always named for the foot, the direction, and the edge (e.g., right forward outside), and always skated on a curve — the tighter the curve, the greater the edge. Pressure cue: outside edge = little-toe side of the blade; inside edge = big-toe side. Golden rule: keep the skating hip aligned with the ankle and shoulder — edges are never done by "ankle skating."*

---

## 1. Forward Outside Edges (Fig. 4.6)

![Feet & blades, from above](edge1-forward-outside-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![Right forward outside edge](fig6-forward-outside.png)

- **Body:** Arms stretched out to the sides, palms facing the ice and pressing down slightly; ribcage lifted so the back stays straight. Lean slightly toward the skating side.
- **Feet:** Start in a **T position** (right foot in front, its heel at the left foot's instep). Both knees bend; push off as the left ankle bends to its inside edge and the weight transfers onto the right foot. The free (left) foot is held behind the skating foot, then swings through — passing close beside it — to end in front, over the print. Repeat on the other foot.
- **Blade:** Right foot on its **outside edge**, on a slight curve. (Diagram: red dot = the pressing outside edge.)
- **Tips:**
  - Circle drill: glide on one foot on a circle — the foot on the inside of the circle rides its outside edge. Practice both directions for right and left edges.
  - Alignment check at the barrier: ankle, hip, and shoulder of the skating side in one straight line.

## 2. Forward Inside Edges (Fig. 4.7)

![Feet & blades, from above](edge2-forward-inside-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![Right forward inside edge](fig7-forward-inside.png)

- **Body:** Held square to the tracing. The arm opposite the skating foot (left arm, for a right edge) is in front of the body, palm down and pressing slightly; the other arm is behind, level with the front arm. Lean slightly toward the inside of the curve (left, for a right inside edge).
- **Feet:** Start in a T position, right foot in front. Both knees bend; push off as the left ankle bends to its inside edge and weight transfers onto the right foot. Free foot held behind, then passes close beside the skating foot to end in front, over the print. Repeat on the other foot.
- **Blade:** Right foot on its **inside edge**, on a slight curve.
- **Tips:**
  - Circle drill: glide on two feet on a circle and pick up the foot on the inside of the circle — the remaining foot is on its inside edge. Skate the circle both clockwise and counterclockwise.

## 3. Back Outside Edges

▶ **Watch:** [How to do Backward Edges on Ice — Coach Julia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OQkMH_YFZDI)

![Feet & blades, from above](edge3-back-outside-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Arms out to the side, palms facing the ice. Left arm in front, right arm behind during the glide.
- **Feet:** Start parallel, shoulder-width apart, knees bent. The knees rise and the weight shifts onto the left foot, which pushes you onto the **right back outside edge**. As the weight transfers, the right foot lifts about **3 inches**, toe pointed inward (pigeon-toed), so it comes down on a backward edge, not a forward one. The free (left) foot is then held above the skating foot, over the print.
- **Blade:** Right foot on its back **outside edge**, on a slight curve. The push-off leaves a curved print nicknamed a **"rat tail."**
- **Tips:**
  - Circle drill: skate backward on a circle with a pump step; the foot on the inside of the circle rides its back outside edge. Both directions.

## 4. Back Inside Edges

▶ **Watch:** [How To Do Back Inside Edges For Beginner Skaters! — Coach Julia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kb0wu6jdzVo)

![Feet & blades, from above](edge4-back-inside-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Arms stretched out to the side; left arm in front, right arm behind the body during the glide.
- **Feet:** Start parallel, shoulder-width apart, knees bent. Weight transfers onto the left foot, which pushes you onto the **right back inside edge**; the right foot rises about 3 inches, toe pointed in, and lands on a backward inside edge. The free foot lifts and is held above the skating foot, over the print. Repeat on the other foot.
- **Blade:** Right foot on its back **inside edge**, on a slight curve.
- **Tips:**
  - Circle drill: glide backward on two feet on a circle; pick up the inside foot — the remaining foot is on its back inside edge. Both directions.

## 5. Change of Edge (Fig. 4.8)

![Feet & blades, from above](edge5-change-of-edge-feet.svg)

*Book photos (a, b, c):*

![Change of edge, start and swing-through](fig8-change-of-edge-ab.png)

![Change of edge, the change itself](fig8-change-of-edge-c.png)

- **Body:** Keep the weight and lean over the **skating hip** the whole time. Shoulders reverse without over-rotating — over-rotation and a broken hip line are the two classic mistakes that make the change impossible.
- **Feet:** One foot stays on the ice throughout. The free foot swings forward, then back (forward changes), driving the rock from one edge to the other; in backward changes it moves back, then forward.
- **Blade:** The blade rocks from one edge to the other through a short **flat** as the curve straightens — the print on the ice is an **S (serpentine)**.
- **Tips:**
  - Four versions: forward outside→inside, forward inside→outside, backward outside→inside, backward inside→outside — practice all of them on both feet.
  - Uses: changing edge before a turn, jump and spin entrances, and recovering when slightly off balance.
  - Practice backward changes on **two feet** first. Advanced standard: the flat part of the S should be no more than one foot long.

## 6. Forward Crossovers (Fig. 4.9)

![Feet & blades, from above](cross1-forward-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![Forward crossovers](fig9-forward-crossovers.png)

- **Body:** Skate on a circle with a lean into it. Arms stay fixed the whole time: for clockwise crossovers, right arm in front and left arm out to the side — like clock hands reading **3:00**.
- **Feet:** With the weight on the **outside edge of the left foot**, the right foot crosses over in front; then the left foot is picked up and placed beside the right. Repeat — and practice the other direction on a counterclockwise circle.
- **Blade:** The crossing (right) foot lands on an **inside edge**; the skating (left) foot rides its outside edge. Speed plus lean is what puts the skates on edge.
- **Tips:**
  - Standing drill: pick up the left foot and cross it in front of the right, then put the right back beside it; step sideways across the ice, then back the other way.
  - Advanced form: every step becomes a stroke — skating knee bent, free leg straight and extended, push from the blade, never the toe picks.

## 7. Back Crossovers

▶ **Watch:** [How to do Backward Crossovers on Figure Skates — Coach Julia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9mhnUmSCTUY)

![Feet & blades, from above](cross2-back-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Same fixed arm positions as forward crossovers (clockwise: right arm in front, left arm extended toward the direction of travel). Skating knee bent; free leg straight and extended.
- **Feet:** Travelling backward on a circle: weight on the **outside edge of the left foot**, the right foot crosses over in front; the left foot is then raised and placed beside the right. Repeat — and practice counterclockwise too.
- **Blade:** Crossing foot lands on an **inside edge**; power comes from edge pressure and knee bend.
- **Tips:**
  - The most common and powerful way to skate backward.
  - Build-up: backward pump step on a circle (neither foot lifts) → stepping one foot over the other with little momentum → add speed.
  - More power: place the inside foot into the circle and draw the feet together before crossing; advanced skaters keep the front foot on the ice and lift only the back foot into the circle — a scissor action.

## Quick comparison

| Skill | Start position | Skating foot rides | Free foot does | Signature drill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forward outside edge | T position | Outside edge, slight curve | Behind → swings through to front | One-foot glide on a circle |
| Forward inside edge | T position | Inside edge, slight curve | Behind → swings through to front | Two-foot circle, lift inside foot |
| Back outside edge | Parallel, shoulder-width | Back outside edge | Held above skating foot, over print | Backward circle, pump step |
| Back inside edge | Parallel, shoulder-width | Back inside edge | Held above skating foot, over print | Backward two-foot circle, lift inside foot |
| Change of edge | Already on an edge | Rocks outside ↔ inside through a flat | Swings forward/back to drive the rock | Two-foot backward changes first |
| Forward crossovers | On a circle, leaning in | Outside edge | Crosses over on its inside edge | Standing step-over drill |
| Back crossovers | On a circle, going backward | Outside edge | Crosses over on its inside edge | Backward pump step on a circle |
