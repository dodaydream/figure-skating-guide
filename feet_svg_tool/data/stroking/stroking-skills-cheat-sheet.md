# Figure Skating Stroking — Cheat Sheet

*From the "Stroking Skills" section of Chapter 4 of* The Complete Book of Figure Skating *(Carole Shulman, pp. 44–48). Note: the book's "sculling" is the move most rinks today call a **swizzle** （葫蘆）. The balance rule that runs through the whole section: forward skating — weight slightly **back** of centre on the blade; backward skating — weight slightly **forward** of centre.*

---

## 1. Forward Sculling (Swizzle)

▶ **Watch:** [How to do forward swizzles - learn to ice skate online — Coach Mary Figure Skating](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QF9QI449SVw)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke1-forward-sculling-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Arms extended out to the sides for balance, knees bent; weight slightly back of centre on the blades (the balance for all forward skating).
- **Feet:** Start with feet together in a V, toes pointed out. Push the feet forward and out to the sides until parallel, slightly more than shoulder-width apart; then straighten the knees and pull the feet back together, toes pointing in, until side by side. Repeat — out, then in — until it flows. (Diagram: the start position at the bottom of the lemon-shaped track the feet trace.)
- **Blade:** The inside edge of each foot presses into the ice to drive the push out.
- **Tips:** Neither foot leaves the ice — a pump step. Be patient; the right pressure makes it an effortless glide.

## 2. Forward Glides — basic stroking (Fig. 4.5)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke2-forward-glides-feet.svg)

*Book photo:*

![Forward stroking](fig5-forward-stroking.png)

- **Body:** Balanced over both feet for the two-foot glide, weight slightly back of centre. Start near the boards until confidence grows.
- **Feet:** Begin parallel, about shoulder-width apart. Bend both knees; one foot (the book uses the right) rotates out to nearly **45°** and presses against the ice, pushing with the entire blade; weight transfers to the other foot, which glides forward on one foot. Bring the feet back together; repeat on the other side.
- **Blade:** The pushing foot drives with the whole blade; the gliding foot runs flat and straight.
- **Tips:** Aim to glide about **three times your height** on one foot. Repeating on the same foot = the one-footed pump step; alternate feet to cross the ice.

## 3. Advanced Forward Stroking

▶ **Watch:** [How To Ice Skate! Best Tutorial For Beautiful Forward Stroking! — Coach Julia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-7QqRz2EZAM)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke3-advanced-forward-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Upper body straight, back erect. A deeper knee and ankle bend before each stroke lengthens it — more power, more speed.
- **Feet:** Begin with feet together. Ankle and knee bend forward; the push-off leg straightens and presses the ice, propelling the skating foot forward. The push-off foot leaves the ice and stretches fully behind the skating foot, knee turned out, toe pointed; then the free leg lowers beside the skating foot, both knees bent, and the stroke repeats on the other side.
- **Blade:** Push from the **inside edge** — never the toe pick. The middle to the ball of the foot presses on the blade.
- **Tips:** The elements are **bend, extension, stretch**. Strength drill: wall sits — feet 12–18 in from a wall, slide down until thighs are perpendicular to the wall, hold 30 seconds (best supervised).

## 4. Power Stroking

▶ **Watch:** [Forward Power Stroking tutorial — Ice Edge Academy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdPqPATTcz4)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke4-power-stroking-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Stroking posture driven explosively — the knee and ankle snap into each push.
- **Feet:** Alternating full strokes, accelerating in as few pushes as possible.
- **Blade:** Explosive push and pressure — feel the blade press into the ice and push away from it.
- **Tips:** Not just pushing quickly — explosive acceleration, top speed in the shortest time. Drill: start on a hockey blue line; on a signal, accelerate to the red line and time it — the time should drop as the stroke strengthens.

## 5. Backward Sculling (Swizzle)

▶ **Watch:** [Learn How to Ice Skate: Backwards Swizzles Lesson 21 — Coach Mary Figure Skating](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_fc6CMS0SWY)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke5-backward-sculling-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Arms stretched out to the sides, knees bent; weight slightly **forward** of centre on the blades (the balance for all backward skating).
- **Feet:** Start with feet together, toes pointed **in** and heels out — the mirror of forward sculling. Press the feet back and out to the sides until parallel, slightly more than shoulder-width apart; straighten the knees and pull the feet back together. Repeat the push–pull pattern. (Diagram: start position at the top of the lemon; red arrow = direction of travel, backward.)
- **Blade:** The inside edge of each foot presses to push the feet apart.
- **Tips:** In-place drill: feet under your body, twist the toes in and out to learn the foot/ankle action; add knee bend and a little momentum.

## 6. Backward Glides

▶ **Watch:** [Learn How to Ice Skate: Backward Glides Lesson 22 — Coach Mary Figure Skating](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BbezbQvyX6I)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke6-backward-glides-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Weight balanced forward over the **ball of the foot** — not onto the toes. Begin near the barrier.
- **Feet:** Short backward steps with toes in, heels out — the backward **"duck walk."** Then a backward two-foot glide, feet close together; when solid, lift one foot and glide on the other.
- **Blade:** Both blades flat for the two-foot glide, weight slightly forward of centre.
- **Tips:** One-foot glide on both feet, aiming for three times your height. Warm-up: the backward wiggle — a hula-hoop action on two feet.

## Bonus: The Snake (Backward Wiggle) — *a teaching tip in the book*

▶ **Watch:** [Backwards Wiggles PART ONE | Ice Skating Moves for Beginners — Coach Ashleigh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZpVTaxvn4c)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke8-snake-wiggle-feet.svg)

*The book mentions this only in a Teaching Tip under Backward Glides ("have skaters try a backward wiggle… a hula hoop action"); the details below follow Learn to Skate–style curricula, where the backward wiggle is the classic first backward movement （蛇行 — "snake" — in the Taiwan association progression, right after the duck walk).*

- **Body:** Knees bent, chest up, arms out to the sides. Balance on the balls of your feet, and let your hips sway side to side, against your shoulders — the book's cue is a hula-hoop action.
- **Feet:** Feet together and parallel, both staying on the ice the whole time. Shift your weight from one foot to the other, pressing out a little with each foot in turn; each small press nudges you backward and your path weaves like a snake.
- **Blade:** Both blades stay down, weight over the balls of the feet; each foot presses out in turn as the weight lands on it.
- **Tips:**
  - Glance over your shoulder before you start — you're travelling where you can't see.
  - If you're wiggling but going nowhere, walk backward slowly with toes turned in to feel the momentum, then go back to wiggling.
  - For kids, the book's image: bunnies wiggling through the carrot patch.

## 7. Advanced Backward Skating

▶ **Watch:** [Unlock Smooth Backward Stroking: Tips and Drills for Every Skater — Style Skating](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rDquL_0cUag)

![Feet & blades, from above](stroke7-advanced-backward-feet.svg)

- **Body:** Weight stays forward; free toe points down; look over one shoulder in the direction of travel — head up, never facing down.
- **Feet:** Push from one foot onto the other (book: right pushes, land on the left). The pushing foot rises just above the ice; the skating knee, slightly bent, straightens slowly as the free foot comes back beside it. Repeat to the other side. (Diagram: dashed boot = free foot hovering.)
- **Blade:** Push and glide on the skating foot; the free foot stays just off the ice.
- **Tips:** Balance is everything: lean too far back → fall; too far forward → the glide stops dead.

## Quick comparison

| Skill | Feet pattern | Weight on blade | Power source | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forward sculling (swizzle) | V → wide → together, repeat | Slightly back of centre | Inside-edge push out, pull in | Forward |
| Forward glides / stroking | Push at ~45°, glide on other foot | Slightly back of centre | Whole-blade push | Forward |
| Advanced forward stroking | Together → push → free leg stretched behind | Slightly back of centre | Inside-edge push, deep bend | Forward |
| Power stroking | Same strokes, explosive | Slightly back of centre | Knee-and-ankle snap | Forward |
| Backward sculling (swizzle) | Toes-in start → back & out → together | Slightly forward of centre | Inside-edge push | Backward |
| Backward glides | Duck walk → two-foot → one-foot glide | Forward, over ball of foot | Short steps, then coast | Backward |
| Advanced backward skating | Push across, free foot hovers | Forward | Alternating backward strokes | Backward |
| Snake / backward wiggle (bonus) | Feet together, weight shifts side to side | Slightly forward, over the balls of the feet | Side-to-side presses + hip sway | Backward |
