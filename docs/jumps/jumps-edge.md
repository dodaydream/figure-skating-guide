# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

Edge jumps are those that **take off from one of the four edges** — forward outside, forward inside, back outside, back inside — with no toe-pick vault. Most skaters prefer either toe jumps or edge jumps; body type, coaching methods, and learning style all influence the preference. This file covers the five edge jumps of Chapter 8 in learning order: Waltz Jump, Salchow, Loop, Walley, and Axel.

::: tip What defines an edge jump
The takeoff comes **straight off the edge**: the skating knee bends, then extends to spring the body into the air — there is no interruption on the takeoff and no vaulting action from a toe tap, which is why edge jumps feel **more flowing** than toe jumps. The toe pick still plays its small part: as on every jump, it is the last part of the blade to leave the ice.
:::

::: info Which rotation are these pages written for?
All descriptions in the book — and in all six Jumps files — assume a skater rotating **left / counter-clockwise**. Skaters who rotate to the right reverse the starting foot and the direction throughout.
:::

## Which jump when?

- [Your first full jump — a half turn, forward takeoff → Waltz Jump](#waltz-jump)
- [One full turn from the back inside edge left by a three turn → Salchow](#salchow)
- [One full turn that takes off and lands on the same back outside edge → Loop](#loop)
- [One full turn off a right back inside edge, landing on the same foot → Walley](#walley)
- [Ready for 1½ turns and one of the few forward-edge takeoffs → Axel](#axel)

## Waltz Jump {#waltz-jump} <Badge type="tip" text="Beginner · ½ rotation" />

A **half-turn rotation** in the air — the first full jump in this set. It takes off on the **left forward outside** edge and lands on the **right back outside** edge. (A seldom-performed one-footed version — same foot, outside-edge takeoff to an inside-edge landing — is sometimes called a three jump.)

![Book photos: waltz jump (a) takeoff, (b) half turn rotation in an open air position, (c) landing](/jumps-edge-assets/fig89-waltz-jump.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.9: Waltz jump: (a) takeoff, (b) half turn rotation in an open air position, and (c) landing*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | **Preparation:** begin on a curve with a left forward outside three turn; the right foot steps backward on a strong back outside edge, then the left foot steps forward for the takeoff. **Takeoff:** the left knee bends and the right (free) leg and both arms extend behind the body (Fig. 8.9a); the body weight rises up off the knee as the free foot swings forward and into the air, and the arms lift to assist in gaining height — leading slightly with the left arm on the rise prevents excessive rotation. **Air position:** at the peak, the legs are in a semi-split or open position with the knees straight and the arms extended out to the side (Fig. 8.9b). **Landing:** the body weight shifts onto the landing foot and centres in its shift from the takeoff to the landing edge. |
| **Feet** | Takeoff on the **left** foot, landing on the **right** — a change of foot. At the landing the toe pick touches first, then the foot rolls back to the ball of the foot. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff edge: **left forward outside**. As in all jumps that take off in a forward direction, the blade rocks from back of centre to front, and the toe pick is the last part of the blade to leave the ice. Landing edge: a flowing **right back outside**. |

#### Tips

- First practice at the barrier: stand perpendicular to the boards, holding on with your left hand and standing on your left foot; jump forward one half turn, change hands on the boards, and land backward on the right foot. Repeat several times before leaving the boards.
- Away from the boards, practise a two-footed jump from front to back before attempting the one-footed jump.

## Salchow {#salchow} <Badge type="info" text="Intermediate · 1 rotation" />

Named for the Swedish world champion **Ulrich Salchow**. It begins from a **back inside edge** and ends on a **back outside edge** — sometimes described as a waltz jump from a back inside edge, which is almost true, except that a waltz jump is one half rotation and the Salchow is **one full revolution**.

![Book photos: Salchow preparation and takeoff positions — (a) control established after the left forward outside three, (b) the right (free) foot brought forward, (c) the free leg moving in a circular pattern around the skating side into the takeoff](/jumps-edge-assets/fig810-salchow.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.10: preparation and takeoff positions in the Salchow. The focus is kept on the positions of the feet during preparation and takeoff, since these are the phases that differ from jump to jump*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | **Preparation:** the entry is begun by a left forward outside three — and this time there is **no change of feet** following the three, as the jump originates from the left back inside edge of the three. Check the shoulders after the three to establish control and set up the momentum of the jump (Fig. 8.10a). **Takeoff:** the right (free) arm moves with the right (free) foot, which is behind the skating foot after the three; both the free arm and the free foot are brought forward as the knee bends (Fig. 8.10b), and the free leg and free arm move in a circular pattern around the skating side (Fig. 8.10c) and into the air position. Rotation is created as the hips come around to face the direction of travel. **Landing:** balance to the ball of the foot on a strong backward glide. |
| **Feet** | Takeoff on the **left** foot, landing on the **right** — a change of foot, with the body weight changing in the air from the left back inside takeoff edge to the right back outside landing edge. On the landing, the toe pick is first to touch the ice. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff edge: **left back inside**. Pressure is maintained on the front of the blade through the takeoff, and the toe pick is the last part of the blade to leave the ice. Landing edge: **right back outside**. |

![Book diagram: the print left on the ice by a well-executed Salchow takeoff, showing the three turn and the takeoff curve](/jumps-edge-assets/fig811-salchow.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.11: print on the ice resulting from a well-executed Salchow takeoff*

#### Tips

- Practise the preparation, including the three, **on a straight line**: after the turn, remain on the line and execute the jump, working on control and balance.

## Loop {#loop} <Badge type="info" text="Intermediate · 1 rotation" />

The loop begins from a **right back outside edge**, rotates **one full revolution**, and lands on the **same edge** it took off from — the right back outside. Its air position is the basic position for body and balance in all multi-rotation jumps; Fritz Dietl named the loop the "king of jumps," and it used to be known as the Rittberger.

![Book photos: loop preparation and takeoff positions — (a) from a right back outside edge, free foot trailing in front, knee bent, (b) the knee begins to straighten, (c) takeoff and rotation begin](/jumps-edge-assets/fig812-loop.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.12: preparation and takeoff positions in the loop jump: (a) from a right back outside edge, (b) the knee begins to straighten in order to (c) begin takeoff and rotation*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | **Preparation** — three common entries: (1) a left forward outside three, then a step down onto the right back outside edge; (2) a right forward inside three ending on the right back outside edge; (3) a right forward inside Mohawk, then a step down onto the right back outside edge. From the back outside edge, with the free foot trailing in front of the takeoff edge, the right knee bends (Fig. 8.12a); the right arm is placed behind the body and the left arm in front. Keep the body square over the takeoff edge without pushing the skating hip too far into the circle — shoulders, hips, and foot all aligned. **Takeoff and rotation:** straighten the knee and rotate the upper body to leave the ice (Fig. 8.12b–c); the right arm moves toward the left and the arms pull together in the air. **Landing:** the free foot lifts slightly, then extends back into the right back outside landing position. |
| **Feet** | Takeoff and landing on the **same (right) foot**. During the jump the free foot remains in front, with the legs slightly crossed — a position that becomes more pronounced as rotations increase. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff edge and landing edge are the same: **right back outside**. |

#### Tips

- Timing is critical: use a 1–2–3 count, or words such as "hold—bend—jump," to establish the rhythm.
- Practise the takeoff and landing positions **without rotation**: jump up in place with the free foot in front and the arms in, then land with the free foot lifting from the front and extending back and the arms stretched out. Practise first on the floor, then with a spotter on the ice.
- A straight-line preparation is helpful to correct an over-rotated takeoff.

## Walley {#walley} <Badge type="warning" text="Intermediate–Advanced · 1 rotation" />

Named for **Nate Walley**. It is executed from a **right back inside edge**, lifting off and rotating **one full turn** in the air before landing on a **right back outside edge** — the same foot it took off from.

![Book photos: Walley preparation and takeoff positions — (a) during the entry, the free foot crossed behind the skating foot, (b) the free foot kicks out to the side, (c) the body begins to rotate counter-clockwise and spring into the air off the back inside edge](/jumps-edge-assets/fig813-walley.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.13: preparation and takeoff positions in the Walley*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | **Preparation** — two common methods: (1) a chain of back inside–outside–inside changes of edge, the last back inside edge held, with the free foot extended behind the skating foot just before the takeoff; (2) a left forward outside three followed by a step down on the right back inside edge. On either entry, use a strong checked position with the left arm in front of the body and the right arm behind; the right (skating) knee is bent. **Takeoff and rotation:** the momentum of the liftoff is generated by the left (free) foot kicking from behind the skating foot and out to the side (Fig. 8.13b), as the arms and the skating knee lift up while the body begins to rotate counter-clockwise (Fig. 8.13c). The body weight must be firmly centred over the right side for the takeoff. In the air the left (free) leg is in an open position, parallel to the right foot and hip-width away; the arms pull in across the chest and open out for the landing. |
| **Feet** | Takeoff and landing on the **same (right) foot**. The left (free) foot is extended and crossed behind the right (skating) foot, outside the line of tracing (Fig. 8.13a), then kicks out to the side to start the liftoff. As the jump springs into the air, the right (skating) foot pushes away from the ice. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff edge: **right back inside**. Landing edge: **right back outside**. |

#### Tips

- Practise alternate jumping side-to-side from a right back inside edge to a left back inside edge, without rotation, to strengthen the takeoff edge.
- Practise a right back inside counter to learn the placement of the weight over the right side on the takeoff.

## Axel {#axel} <Badge type="danger" text="Advanced · 1½ rotations" />

Named for Norwegian **Axel Paulsen**, who invented the jump in **1882**; it was first performed in competition in Vienna, Austria, and in its day it was the most difficult jump ever performed. The Axel is considered a single jump, but it is actually **one and one-half revolutions** in the air — and it is one of the few jumps that take off on a **forward edge**: it starts on the **left forward outside** edge and lands on the **right back outside** edge.

::: warning Before an Axel is attempted
- All single jumps landed soundly
- Single jump combinations landed with balance and strength — e.g., flip/loop, loop/loop, Lutz/loop, and waltz/loop
- Backspins executed strongly
:::

![Book photo: the Axel takeoff position — arms and free leg extended behind the body on the left forward outside edge, free knee bent](/jumps-edge-assets/fig814-axel.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.14: the Axel takes off from a forward outside edge*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | **Preparation:** the most common is a left forward outside three turn, stepping back on a right back outside edge and then stepping forward on the left outside edge. Forward outside swing rolls can also be used, or an entry from back crossovers, stepping down on a right back outside edge before turning forward on the left foot. **Takeoff:** the arms and the free leg are extended behind the body on the takeoff edge (Fig. 8.14), the skating knee well bent; the extended free leg is held solidly in back to control excessive rotation. On the takeoff the free leg must swing **straight through, not around**, with the free knee bent. The left foot rolls up to the toe pick and springs into the air as the left knee straightens and the right knee and arms thrust forward to help get the jump to its full height; the arms pass close to the body from back to front, bending at the elbows. **Air position:** the same as for the loop jump — by the apex, the body has turned one half rotation; the arms pull in across the centre of the chest with the elbows down, and the left hip is slightly lifted. **Landing:** the arms open out into a checked landing position. |
| **Feet** | Takeoff on the **left** foot, landing on the **right** — a change of foot, with the body weight shifting in the air from the left (takeoff) foot to the right (landing) foot. In the air the left leg is crossed in front. For the landing, the left leg lifts slightly and passes close by the right (landing) leg, then the left (free) leg is extended fully behind, with the right (landing) knee bent. |
| **Blade** | Takeoff edge: **left forward outside** — expect a slight skid or scrape on the takeoff edge. Keep the skid to a minimum: too much skid affects the quality of the jump, and the body position and early rotation must be corrected. Landing edge: **right back outside**. |

![Book diagram: the print left on the ice by a well-executed Axel takeoff — the RBO entry curve into the LFO takeoff](/jumps-edge-assets/fig815-axel.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.15: print on the ice resulting from a well-executed Axel takeoff*

#### Tips

- The **bell jump** is a good practice exercise: an Axel executed with just one rotation and landed forward on two feet, to feel the body and air positions without having to concentrate on the landing.
- Do several single loop jumps in a row to feel the tighter body position needed in the rotation of the Axel.
- Waltz/loop-type combination practice builds the landing action of an Axel from jumps you already have.

## All five edge jumps, side by side

| Jump | Takeoff edge | Rotations | Landing edge | Same foot or change of foot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Waltz Jump | Left forward outside | ½ | Right back outside | Change of foot |
| Salchow | Left back inside | 1 | Right back outside | Change of foot |
| Loop | Right back outside | 1 | Right back outside | Same foot |
| Walley | Right back inside | 1 | Right back outside | Same foot |
| Axel | Left forward outside | 1½ | Right back outside | Change of foot |

Every jump on this page lands on a **right back outside edge** — the standard landing from [Jumps — Fundamentals](./jumps-fundamentals.md). What separates the five is the takeoff edge and the number of rotations.
