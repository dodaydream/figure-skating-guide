# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

Turns are how skating changes direction without losing flow — and footwork is where the turns become a performance. From Chapter 6 of *The Complete Book of Figure Skating*: three turns, double threes, Mohawks, Choctaws, brackets, counters, rockers, and the step sequences that link them.

::: tip The naming rule
Every turn changes direction. What **else** changes names the turn — a **three turn** changes edge, a **Mohawk** changes feet, a **Choctaw** changes both, a **bracket** changes edge but turns against the circle, and a **counter** or **rocker** changes neither: same edge, new circle.
:::

## Which turn when?

- [Your first front-to-back turn → Inside Open Mohawk](#inside-open-mohawk)
- [Turn on one foot, no foot change → Three Turns](#three-turns)
- [Two threes, no step down → Double Threes](#double-threes)
- [A step that changes edge too → Choctaws](#choctaws)
- [The turn that fights the circle → Brackets](#brackets)
- [Same edge, new circle, turn faces out → Counters](#counters)
- [Same edge, new circle, turn faces in → Rockers](#rockers)
- [Put it all together → Footwork](#footwork)

How to read the diagrams: each turn is shown in three keyframes — **1 · Entry, 2 · The turn, 3 · Exit** — looking down at your own feet. Red dot = the edge doing the work; pale blue = the print left on the ice; a dashed boot is the free foot, lifted off the ice. The Mohawk strips carry a red direction arrow: a Mohawk turns you from forward to backward on one smooth curve, so the print alone doesn't show the change.

## Three Turns <Badge type="tip" text="Foundation" /> {#three-turns}

One foot, on a curve, turning 180° — front to back or back to front. It changes **edge and direction, never feet**, and the print it leaves looks like the numeral **3**, its cusp pointing into the circle.

::: info The four variants
- Forward outside → back inside
- Forward inside → back outside
- Back outside → forward inside
- Back inside → forward outside
:::

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Rotate the arms and upper body over the skating foot, then **check** — stop the rotation dead at the top of the turn or the exit edge spins away. Skating knee bent on entry, rises through the turn, bent again on exit. Head turns with the body into the new direction. |
| **Feet** | The free foot stays over the print the whole way — **behind** the skating foot (to the heel) for forward threes, **in front** for back threes. |
| **Blade** | Balance starts just back of centre on a forward edge and rocks forward to just behind the first toe pick at the turn — never onto the pick. On back threes it starts just forward of centre and rocks back. |

#### Tips

- Learn it in this order: at the barrier → two-footed on a circle → one foot. A forward outside three (RFO → RBI) is the classic first one.
- For the back inside three, think "sweep the rainbow" with the free foot.

## Double Threes <Badge type="warning" text="Intermediate" /> {#double-threes}

Two three turns linked on the same foot with **no step down in between**: forward → back → forward again — e.g. an RFO three to RBI, straight into a second three back to RFO.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Everything from the three turn, plus discipline: the whole skill is **checking the exit of the first turn** before you allow the second rotation to start. Rushed doubles wobble; checked doubles flow. |
| **Feet** | One foot throughout; the free foot never touches down and stays over the print. |
| **Blade** | The edge flips with each turn — outside → inside → outside (or the reverse) — balance rocking to the front of the blade at each cusp. |

#### Tips

- On a circle, the two turns sit in equal thirds — start at 6 o'clock, turn at 10 and at 2, cusps pointing at the centre like a Y. If the rotation won't behave, practise on two feet, or on a straight line, before going back to the circle.
- **Drop three** (dance term): a forward outside three with an instant step — the feet come together at the point of the three *without* the free foot touching down, both knees bend, and the new foot pushes off onto a back outside edge.

## Inside Open Mohawk <Badge type="tip" text="Beginner–Intermediate" /> {#inside-open-mohawk}

The most common turn in skating — the everyday way to go from forward to backward. A two-foot turn: it changes **feet and direction, not edge** (right forward inside → left back inside). "Open" because the free foot is placed heel-to-instep and the hips finish open, free foot behind.

| **Body** | Shoulders rotate into the turn, then check; step down onto a bent knee. Hips stay open — at the exit the new free foot is behind you. |
| **Feet** | The free foot starts **behind** the skating foot; bring its **heel to the instep** of the skating foot, then step down toward the ball of the foot. |
| **Blade** | Inside edge before, inside edge after — only the foot and the direction change. |

#### Tips

- Practise the placement standing at the barrier (heel-to-heel stepping). A spread-eagle two-foot step teaches your hips the open position.

- If your free foot keeps ending up behind, you're doing the open one — check the placement: heel-to-instep = open, instep-to-heel = closed.

#### Tips — the rest of the Mohawk family

- **Back Mohawk:** the simple step from a back edge to the *same* edge forward, with no change of circle (if the edge or circle changes, it's a Choctaw).
- **Drop Mohawk:** brings the feet together right after the turn, making three steps of it — it appears in the eight-step Mohawk.
- **Swing Mohawk:** swings the free foot past the skating foot before placing it.

## Brackets <Badge type="danger" text="Advanced" /> {#brackets}

The three turn's opposite twin. One foot, changes edge and direction like a three — but where a three rotates **into** the circle, a bracket turns **outside** it. The rotation is exactly reversed, and the print's cusp points away from the circle's centre.

- The **advanced back bracket** adds a scissor: the free foot swings forward as the skating foot moves back, for depth and counter.

## Counters <Badge type="danger" text="Advanced" /> {#counters}

One foot, and the edge never changes: **forward inside → back inside, forward outside → back outside**. Like a change of edge with a turn in the middle — except the turn faces into the **new** circle, taking you off your original circle entirely.

- If you find you've changed edge, you've skated a bracket or a three — a counter keeps its edge at all costs.

## Rockers <Badge type="danger" text="Advanced" /> {#rockers}

## Footwork <Badge type="info" text="All levels" /> {#footwork}

Turns plus freestyle moves, combined — **"the glue that holds a program together."** A step sequence is built in one of three patterns, and judges read the rulebook words literally: **bold, at least, full, complete, must, not less**.

![Book photo: Scott Hamilton performing a footwork sequence](/turns-assets/hamilton-footwork.png)
*From the book — 'Master the Move': Scott Hamilton's footwork (photo © Anthony Neste)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Straight line** | End to end of the rink, down the full long axis or corner to corner. |
| **Circular** | One complete circle or oval using the full width of the ice. |
| **Serpentine** | End to end in at least two bold curves, each no less than half the rink's width. (Spiral sequences follow the same pattern rules.) |

#### What makes it good

- Carriage and style; speed and flow; quick, sure, deep, clean edges; rhythm and timing with the music; changes of rotational direction; highs and lows.
- A variety of complex turns — threes, Mohawks, Choctaws, brackets, counters, rockers — linked with spirals, lunges, half jumps, and creative steps.

#### Master the Move — Scott Hamilton's lesson

- Creativity, expression, and personality count alongside strong edges — movements in both directions, surprise elements, clean quick sure edges, and skating *to* the music, not just on it. Character is what separates doing the steps from performing them.

## All seven turns at a glance

| Turn | Feet | Changes edge? | Changes feet? | Rotation | Print clue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three turn | One | Yes | No | Into the circle | A "3", cusp pointing in |
| Double three | One | Yes, twice | No | Into the circle, twice | Two linked 3s — thirds of a circle, like a Y |
| Mohawk | Two (a step) | No | Yes | — | One smooth arc, no cusp |
| Choctaw | Two (a step) | Yes | Yes | Hips twist | Steps onto a new circle |
| Bracket | One | Yes | No | **Outside** the circle | Cusp pointing out |
| Counter | One | No | No | Outside the original circle | Turn faces the new circle |
| Rocker | One | No | No | Into the original circle | Turn faces the original circle |
