# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

Every jump in Chapter 8 of *The Complete Book of Figure Skating* is built the same way — four phases, in the same order, with the same air position and the same landing. What changes from jump to jump is only the preparation and the takeoff. This file is the framework the other five Jumps files hang on: direction of rotation first, then the four phases, then how the jumps group into families and the order to learn them in.

::: tip The one rule before any jump
**Jump and spin in the same direction.** Always. A skater who jumps one way and spins the other has to fix it at the beginning level — skaters who are inclined to rotate both ways wait until all the basics are learned before introducing the alternate rotation.
:::

::: info Which rotation are these pages written for?
All descriptions in the book — and in all six Jumps files — assume a skater rotating **left / counter-clockwise**. Skaters who rotate to the right reverse the starting foot and the direction throughout.
:::

## Determining the Direction of Rotation <Badge type="info" text="Before you start" /> {#determining-the-direction-of-rotation}

Clockwise (to the right) or counter-clockwise (to the left) — most skaters find their natural direction without help. For those who are confused, jump one way then the other, or jump and spin in opposite directions, the book gives three checks. Direction seldom relates to whether the skater is right- or left-handed.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Turn test: the skater moves away, someone calls their name, and the direction they naturally turn to respond is noted. Repeat the exercises several times to check consistency — especially with skaters who might be ambidextrous. |
| **Feet** | **The stronger leg decides.** All basic jumps land on the same foot, so find the stronger leg first — it becomes the landing leg. Check which leg kicks a ball, and which foot goes first when climbing a stair. |
| **Blade** | Once the direction is set, every jump and every spin uses it — the entry edges, the rotation, and the landing are all built around it. |

#### Tips

- Rotating to the left carries a real advantage: it is the direction most skaters use, and it is the natural directional flow used in all rinks.
- Do not learn the alternate rotation early. Wait until all the basics are learned first.

## The Four Phases <Badge type="tip" text="Foundation" /> {#the-four-phases}

Every jump has exactly four key parts: **1 · Preparation, 2 · Takeoff, 3 · Rotation (air position), 4 · Landing**. Precise timing is critical in all of them — each body movement must happen at an exact time and place, and all the body parts must work harmoniously together to create rhythm.

![Book diagram: stick-figure sequences showing the phases of (a) the double toe loop and (b) the double Axel, each labelled preparation, takeoff, air position and landing](/jumps-fundamentals-assets/fig81-jump-phases.png)
*From the book — Figure 8.1: jump phases of the (a) double toe loop and (b) double Axel*

::: info What Figure 8.1 is really showing
While the preparation and takeoff positions differ from jump to jump, **the air position and landing are the same for all jumps**. The only difference is the tightness of the body in the air, depending on the number of rotations.
:::

### 1 · Preparation

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | What the body, arms, and legs do to get ready for *this* jump — each jump has its own specific positions, movements, and patterns. Learn them clearly and do them correctly **and exactly the same every time**. A good rhythm in the preparation assists the liftoff. Keep the back straight going into any jump. |
| **Feet** | Set by the specific jump — see its card on the Preparatory, Edge, or Toe file. |
| **Blade** | Set by the specific jump — the entry edge is part of the preparation. |

#### Tips

- The common error to watch: **rotating the shoulders and hips before the jump**. The rotation belongs in the air, not in the setup.

### 2 · Takeoff

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | The **knees are the main explosive force**, assisted by the arms, ankles, feet, upper body, head, and correct body alignment. To get maximum height the body must be straight — and must not rotate excessively before the jump. Rhythm and timing between all the body parts matter more than any single push. |
| **Feet** | On **toe jumps**, the timing between the skating leg and the toe tap is critical — the tap also initiates the rotation. On **edge jumps**, the skating knee bends before jumping and then extends to a straight leg on the takeoff. |
| **Blade** | The **toe pick should be the last contact with the ice**, on edge jumps as well as toe jumps. On edge jumps, rotation begins as the curve of the takeoff edge increases. |

### 3 · Rotation — the air position

The same for every jump, from a bunny hop to a triple Axel.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | The body rotates **on an axis** — straight from the head to the toes. Hips and shoulders level and square. The tighter the arms and free leg are held, the more rotations fit in: the greater the number of rotations, the tighter everything must be. |
| **Feet** | Legs straight and **tightly crossed at the ankles**, with the knees and feet facing straight ahead. |
| **Blade** | Off the ice — the only phase where the blade does nothing. What set the rotation going was the takeoff: the toe tap on toe jumps, the increasing curve of the edge on edge jumps. |

#### Tips

- A **wrapped free leg is undesirable** — and it is caused mainly by premature rotation of the shoulders and hips back in the preparation. Fix the wrap before the jump, not in the air.

### 4 · Landing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Landing means **checking** — stopping the rotation by quickly extending the arms and free leg into their landing positions. Hips square, free leg extended, upper body strong. A good landing position has a **softly bent knee** to cushion the weight, a stretched and extended free foot with the free leg turned out, a **straight back**, a **raised head**, and arms extended out to the side — arm positions vary slightly with style, and to gain control or correct over-rotation. |
| **Feet** | Land on the **toe of the blade**, then settle back and stay balanced over the **ball of the foot** for maximum glide. |
| **Blade** | A strong **back outside edge** — quiet and smooth, and **not on a tight curve**. |

## Jumping Progression — the families <Badge type="info" text="All levels" /> {#jumping-progression}

Categorizing jumps by their takeoff groups them into **families**. It shows the similarities and differences in execution — and it is why this chapter is split into one file per family.

| Family | Jumps |
|---|---|
| **Edge jumps** — take off from an edge | Bunny hop, waltz jump, Axel, Salchow, loop, Walley |
| **Toe jumps** — take off with a toe-pick vault | Split, flip, toe loop, Lutz, toe Walley, stag jump |

Two other groupings cut across the families:

| Grouping | Jumps |
|---|---|
| **Take off and land on the same foot** | Loop, flip, Lutz |
| **Change feet** | Bunny hop, waltz jump, Axel, Salchow, toe loop |

#### The learning order

- The **bunny hop** is often the first jump introduced — most skaters can do it off either foot, and its takeoff and springing action carry straight through to the waltz jump and later to Axels. Repeated bunny hops the length of the rink strengthen the jumping leg and teach the timing of the arms and free leg.
- **Half jumps** are valuable next: they teach the action and timing needed for single jumps before full rotation is added.
- **Small jumps** such as mazurkas and falling leaves establish rhythm in jumping, and help develop footwork and jumping sequences.
- The usual order for teaching **single jumps is Salchow, toe loop, loop, flip, and Lutz** — but there are no hard and fast rules.
- Each jump should be taught with a particular pattern and setup, and that rhythm pattern and setup can be carried through singles, doubles, triples, and quads.
