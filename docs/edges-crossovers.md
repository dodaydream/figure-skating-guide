# {{ $frontmatter.title }}

Edges are the foundation of figure skating — speed, balance, lean, and control all come from them — and crossovers are how you carry those edges around a corner. From Chapter 4 of *The Complete Book of Figure Skating*.

::: tip The naming & alignment rule
Every edge is named for the foot, the direction, and the edge — e.g., **right forward outside** — and every edge is skated on a curve: the tighter the curve, the greater the edge. Keep the skating hip in line with the ankle and shoulder; edges are never done by 'ankle skating' alone.
:::

::: warning The eight edges
There are eight edges: **outside and inside × right and left feet × forward and backward**. Pressure cue: outside edge = the little-toe side of the blade presses; inside edge = the big-toe side.
:::

## Which skill when?

| When | Skill |
|------|-------|
| Glide on one foot, going forward | [Forward Outside](#forward-outside-edges) & [Inside Edges](#forward-inside-edges) |
| Glide on one foot, going backward | [Back Outside](#back-outside-edges) & [Inside Edges](#back-inside-edges) |
| Switch edges without putting a foot down | [Change of Edge](#change-of-edge) |
| Around the corner, forward | [Forward Crossovers](#forward-crossovers) |
| Around the corner, backward | [Back Crossovers](#back-crossovers) |

Reading the diagrams: in every diagram you are looking down at your own feet. The red arrow points in the direction of travel (for backward skills it points down the page, toward you). The pale blue line is the curve your blade prints on the ice; a red dot marks the pressing edge. A dashed boot is the free foot, lifted off the ice. The change of edge and the crossovers are shown in three keyframes — entry, the key moment, and exit.

*From the book — Figure 4.6: right forward outside edge*

| | |
|---|---|
| Body | Arms stretched out to the sides, palms facing the ice and pressing down slightly; ribcage lifted so the back stays straight. Lean slightly toward the skating side. |
| Feet | Start in a T position (right foot in front, its heel at the left foot's instep). Both knees bend; push off as the left ankle bends to its inside edge and the weight transfers onto the right foot. The free (left) foot is held behind the skating foot, then swings through — passing close beside it — to end in front, over the print. Repeat on the other foot. |
| Blade | The right foot rides its outside edge on a slight curve. (In the diagram, the red dot is the pressing outside edge.) |

#### Tips

- Circle drill: glide on one foot on a circle — the foot on the inside of the circle rides its outside edge. Practice both directions to get right and left edges.
- Alignment check at the barrier: ankle, hip, and shoulder of the skating side in one straight, vertical line.

| | |
|---|---|
| Body | Arms out to the side, palms facing the ice; during the glide, the left arm is in front and the right arm behind. |
| Feet | Start parallel, shoulder-width apart, knees bent. The knees rise and the weight shifts onto the left foot, which pushes you onto the right back outside edge. As the weight transfers, the right foot lifts about 3 inches, toe pointed inward (pigeon-toed), so it comes down on a backward edge rather than a forward one. The free (left) foot is then held above the skating foot, over the print. Repeat on the other foot. |
| Blade | The right foot rides its back outside edge on a slight curve. The push-off leaves a curved print nicknamed a "rat tail." |

#### Tips

- Circle drill: skate backward on a circle with a pump step and feel the back outside edge — the foot on the inside of the circle rides it. Both directions.

*From the book — Figure 4.8: (a) start on a right outside edge, (b) bring the left foot forward, (c) the right foot changes to an inside edge*

| | |
|---|---|
| Body | Keep the weight and lean over the skating hip the whole time. The shoulders reverse without over-rotating — over-rotation, and breaking the skating hip's alignment, are the two classic mistakes that make the change impossible. |
| Feet | One foot stays on the ice throughout. The free foot swings forward, then back (in forward changes), driving the rock from one edge to the other; in backward changes it moves back, then forward. |
| Blade | The blade rocks from one edge to the other through a short flat as the curve straightens — the print it leaves on the ice is an S (serpentine). |

#### Tips

- Four versions: forward outside→inside, forward inside→outside, backward outside→inside, backward inside→outside — practice all of them, on both feet.
- Where it's used: changing edge before a turn, jump and spin entrances, and recovering when you're slightly off balance.
- Practice backward changes on two feet first. Advanced standard: the flat part of the S should be no more than one foot long.

![Black-and-white book photo in two panels: (a) weight on the outside edge of the left foot, (b) the right foot crossing over on an inside edge](/edges-assets/fig9-forward-crossovers.png)

*From the book — Figure 4.9: (a) weight on the outside edge of the left foot, (b) the right foot crosses over on an inside edge*

| | |
|---|---|
| Body | Skate on a circle with a lean into it. The arms stay fixed the whole time: for clockwise crossovers, right arm in front and left arm out to the side — like clock hands reading 3:00 — whichever foot the weight is on. |
| Feet | With the weight on the outside edge of the left foot, the right foot crosses over in front; then the left foot is picked up and placed beside the right. Repeat — and practice the other direction on a counterclockwise circle. |
| Blade | The crossing (right) foot lands on an inside edge; the skating (left) foot rides its outside edge. Speed plus lean is what puts the skates on edge. |

#### Tips

- Standing drill: pick up the left foot and cross it in front of the right, then put the right back beside it; step sideways across the ice, then back the other way, until your balance is secure.
- Advanced form: every step becomes a stroke — skating knee bent, free leg straight and extended, push from the blade, never the toe picks.

| | |
|---|---|
| Body | The same fixed arm positions as forward crossovers (clockwise: right arm in front, left arm extended toward the direction of travel). Skating knee bent; free leg straight and extended. |
| Feet | Travelling backward on a circle: weight on the outside edge of the left foot, the right foot crosses over in front; the left foot is then raised and placed beside the right. Repeat — and practice counterclockwise too. |
| Blade | The crossing foot lands on an inside edge; power comes from edge pressure and knee bend. |

#### Tips

- The most common and powerful way of skating backward.
- Build-up: backward pump step on a circle (neither foot lifts) → stepping one foot over the other with little momentum → add speed.
- More power: place the inside foot into the circle and draw the feet together before crossing; advanced skaters keep the front foot on the ice and lift only the back foot into the circle — a scissor action.

## All seven skills at a glance

| Skill | Start position | Skating foot rides | Free foot does | Signature drill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forward outside edge | T position | Outside edge, slight curve | Behind → swings through to front | One-foot glide on a circle |
| Forward inside edge | T position | Inside edge, slight curve | Behind → swings through to front | Two-foot circle, lift the inside foot |
| Back outside edge | Parallel, shoulder-width | Back outside edge | Held above the skating foot, over the print | Backward circle, pump step |
| Back inside edge | Parallel, shoulder-width | Back inside edge | Held above the skating foot, over the print | Backward two-foot circle, lift the inside foot |
| Change of edge | Already on an edge | Rocks outside ↔ inside through a flat | Swings forward/back to drive the rock | Two-foot backward changes first |
| Forward crossovers | On a circle, leaning in | Outside edge | Crosses over on its inside edge | Standing step-over drill |
| Back crossovers | On a circle, going backward | Outside edge | Crosses over on its inside edge | Backward pump step on a circle |
